# Fostering Collective Intelligence in Human-AI Collaboration: COHUMAIN

**Authors:** Pranav Gupta (U. Illinois), Thuy Ngoc Nguyen & Cleotilde Gonzalez (Carnegie Mellon), Anita Williams Woolley (Carnegie Mellon)

## Core Framework: COHUMAIN

Collective Human-Machine Intelligence (COHUMAIN) is an interdisciplinary research domain addressing: "How do we know that a sociotechnical system as a whole is exhibiting collective intelligence?"

### Four Core Problems (P1-P4)

**Between-Member Metacognition:**
- P1: How members perceive each other's mental states and shape their own cognition
- P3: How members recognize distributed cognitive resources and specialized knowledge

**Between-Member Interactions:**
- P2: How members align diverse mental states through trustworthy interactions
- P4: How members coordinate actions using shared norms for resource utilization

## The Transactive Systems Model (TSM-CI)

Three interlocking systems:

### 1. Transactive Memory System (TMS)
Manages distributed knowledge and specializations. AI enhances this by accelerating discovery of expertise and facilitating skill-sharing connections between collaborators.

### 2. Transactive Attention System (TAS)
Coordinates members' limited attention resources. Caution: AI tools managing attention entirely risk "undermining collective cognition and performance" when human oversight remains feasible.

### 3. Transactive Reasoning System (TRS)
Aligns collective goals with environmental changes. AI can prompt reasoning exchanges when goal misalignment emerges.

## Instance-Based Learning Theory (IBLT)

IBLT is proposed as a compatible cognitive architecture for AI agents because it:
- Encodes actions as memory instances with state-action-utility triplets
- Supports bounded rationality consistent with human decision-making
- Enables machine theory of mind (MToHM) development

IBLT-based models predict human behavior more accurately than Bayesian approaches.

## Key Research Findings

### On Human-AI Trust
- Existing research emphasizes AI competence; benevolence and affective dimensions remain underexplored
- Goal alignment perception strongly influences trust development
- Trust repair mechanisms in human-AI relationships remain poorly understood

### On Human-Machine Interaction
- Human-only teams consistently outperform human-AI teams due to more efficient information sharing
- Moderate agent autonomy often proves more effective than extremes
- Shared situational awareness between humans and agents is essential

## Potential Harms

Sophisticated AI tools can undermine collective intelligence when they:
- Displace human memory formation (dashboard studies)
- Remove autonomy through micromanagement
- Create over-reliance on AI facilitation

**Goal Autonomy Assumption**: Unlike humans, AI agents lack higher-order goal autonomy. Their ultimate objectives are set by designers, potentially misaligning with human collaborators' interests.

## Conclusions

Effective human-AI collaboration requires:
1. AI with compatible cognitive architectures interfacing with collective-level sociocognitive systems
2. Mechanisms for mutual metacognition and engagement rules
3. AI that enhances rather than displaces human cognitive processes
4. Human oversight and control over technological mediation
