# Spaced Repetition Systems: Comprehensive Overview

## Core Principles

Spaced repetition combats our natural forgetting pattern (the forgetting curve). "Without reinforcement, we lose about 50% of new information within an hour and 90% within a week." The system works through adaptive scheduling: successful recalls trigger longer review intervals, while struggled items return sooner. This moves information from short-term to long-term memory efficiently.

Hermann Ebbinghaus first reported the spacing effect in 1885 — the idea that it's more efficient to space out learning over time as opposed to cramming.

## Active Recall Integration

Active recall amplifies spaced repetition's effectiveness by forcing the brain to retrieve information rather than passively reviewing. This strengthens neural pathways and reveals understanding gaps that passive review misses.

## Knowledge Structure Preservation

A critical challenge: breaking books into flashcard pieces risks losing sight of how concepts interconnect. The solution involves structural cards:
- **Hierarchy cards**: Visual representations of main sections
- **Connection cards**: Testing relationships between concepts
- **Sequence cards**: Testing understanding of staged processes
- **Context cards**: Showing where information fits within arguments

## Anki as the Standard Tool

Anki remains "the most sophisticated spaced repetition platform available, with a proven algorithm and extensive customization options." The FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) algorithm represents a significant advancement, using machine learning to optimize review intervals based on individual performance patterns.

The learning system uses graduated intervals: new cards appear frequently until mastery, then transition to longer intervals, with struggling cards returning to shorter schedules.

## Card Creation Framework

The "minimum information" principle: one clear question with one specific answer. Types include:
- **Concept cards**: Testing definitional understanding
- **Evidence cards**: Pairing studies with demonstrations
- **Application cards**: Testing practical understanding

## Integration with PKM

"The best spaced repetition system is the one you actually use. A simple system used daily beats a complex system used sporadically." Spaced repetition bridges the gap between understanding and retention — while PKM systems like Zettelkasten build connections and generate insights, spaced repetition ensures you can actually recall the knowledge when needed.
