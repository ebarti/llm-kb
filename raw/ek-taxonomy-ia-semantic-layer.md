# Taxonomy and Information Architecture for the Semantic Layer

## Core Definitions

**Semantic Layer**: A standardized framework that organizes and abstracts organizational data (structured, unstructured, semi-structured) and serves as a connector between data and knowledge sources. It functions as a middle layer between content/data repositories and front-end applications, enabling users to search across multiple systems simultaneously.

**Taxonomy**: A controlled vocabulary based on unambiguous concepts structured into hierarchies of broader-narrower relationships, primarily used to tag content for improved discoverability and retrieval.

**Information Architecture (IA)**: "The structural design of shared information environments" including organization, labeling, search, and navigation systems.

## Key Benefits of Semantic Layers
- Unified search across disparate data and content sources
- Consistent metadata and business meaning
- Simplified data access without extensive technical knowledge
- Faster insight retrieval through abstraction of underlying complexity
- Seamless integration of new data sources
- Foundation for reliable AI/ML model development

## Relationship Between Taxonomy and IA
Navigation labels align with search refinements; taxonomy concepts integrate with metadata schemas to provide controlled values; glossaries align with taxonomies through full definitions; ontologies connect multiple taxonomies with semantic relationships. IA transcends presentation layers — it operates across all information management levels.

## Taxonomy's Operational Roles
1. **Consistent Naming**: Standardized terminology across systems
2. **Semantic Context**: Subject domain understanding for concepts
3. **Disambiguation**: Critical preparation for AI use cases
4. **Front/Back End Alignment**: Not all taxonomy concepts display to users; IA determines visibility

## Semantic Layer Architecture Components
- Business glossaries
- Controlled metadata
- Data catalogs
- Taxonomies and thesauri
- Ontologies and knowledge graphs

## Governance
Taxonomies must be managed outside individual system implementations. Best practice recommends building on W3C SKOS (Simple Knowledge Organization System) standards.
