# Evergreen Notes — Andy Matuschak

## Core Definition

Evergreen notes are "written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects." They represent an unusual approach distinguishing them from transient note-taking, focusing on developing insight rather than merely capturing information.

## Five Core Principles

1. **Atomic Structure**: Notes should be focused enough to complete in under 30 minutes, representing single, self-contained ideas.

2. **Concept-Oriented**: Notes are factored by concept rather than by author, book, or project, enabling connections across domains as they're updated over time.

3. **Densely Linked**: Rich associative networks between notes create relationships across the knowledge base, promoting discovery and synthesis.

4. **Associative Ontologies Over Hierarchies**: The system favors connection-based organization rather than rigid categorical structures.

5. **Written for Self, Not Audience**: Notes prioritize personal clarity and thinking development over external presentation or assumed readership.

## Distinctions from Other Systems

**Versus Transient Notes**: Most people create temporary notes that don't accumulate into meaningful insight. Evergreen notes deliberately build intellectual capital over time.

**Versus Zettelkasten**: While heavily inspired by Niklas Luhmann's Zettelkasten, Matuschak uses the distinct term to explore variations independent of existing Zettelkasten cultural associations.

**Versus Spaced Repetition Systems**: Memory systems and evergreen note systems serve different purposes — the former aids retention, the latter builds ideas. Existing spaced repetition tools discourage the incremental synthesis that evergreen notes require.

## Implementation Strategies

- Maintain separate systems: reading inbox for references, writing inbox for developing transient notes into evergreen ones
- Write about readings to internalize texts deeply
- Use "incremental writing" to make progress on complex ideas gradually
- Maintain rich associative structures through regular revision and linking

## Key Properties

**Insight Accumulation**: By externalizing serious thinking in durable form, creative work stops dissipating and instead compounds over time.

**Smooth Progress**: The atomic nature permits regular checkpoints where each completed note represents solid intellectual ground.

**Feedback Loops**: Constant revision and consultation of past notes provides tighter feedback than traditional writing practices.

**Safe Development Space**: Wild ideas can develop incrementally as atomic notes without demanding immediate comprehensive treatment.

**Skill Development**: The practice approximates spaced repetition through maintenance while supporting deeper conceptual synthesis than memory-focused systems.

## Knowledge Work Implications

"Better note-taking" misses the essential point; what matters is "better thinking." The metric for knowledge workers should be "the number of evergreen notes written per day" as a leading indicator of intellectual development.

The system directly counters how most knowledge workers operate: they either store notes passively or use brainstorming as a substitute for systematic idea development. Evergreen notes provide the missing infrastructure for deliberately accumulating insights.
