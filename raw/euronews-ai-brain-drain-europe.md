# The AI Brain Drain: Why Europe Can't Keep the Talent It Trains

## Talent Flow Metrics

**Per Capita Comparison:**
- Europe has about 30% more AI talent per capita than the US and nearly three times as many as China, yet still experiences substantial net outflows of senior professionals.

**Inflow Decline:**
- Net tech talent inflows to Europe collapsed from approximately 52,000 in 2022 to just 26,000 in 2024.

**International Composition:**
- 57% of European AI professionals completed undergraduate studies outside Europe (compared to 38% in the US)
- Ireland: ~28% of AI professionals have Indian undergraduate degrees
- UK: ~14% have Indian undergraduate degrees

## Salary Disparities

**United States vs. Europe:**
- US salaries are typically 30% to 70% higher than in most of Europe
- US mid-to-senior AI engineers: base salaries of $140,000-$210,000
- Western/Northern Europe senior engineers: $90,000-$150,000
- Southern/Eastern Europe: often below $100,000

## Regional Brain Drain Patterns
- Switzerland and smaller countries attracted ~18% of intra-European relocations (2023), primarily from France, Germany, Italy
- Netherlands and UK historically captured ~23% of such moves
- Germany faces "persistent outflow of senior talent" to US, UK, and Switzerland

## Primary Destinations
Most departures target: United States, United Kingdom, Gulf countries, Canada, Australia

## Global AI Talent Shortage (2026)
- Demand exceeds supply by 3.2:1 across key roles
- AI specialists top the list of fastest-growing occupations, with 40% annual growth projected through 2030
- 72% of employers report hiring difficulty (ManpowerGroup 2026)
- Young, highly cited scholars are 100 times more likely to move to industry than ten-year veterans

## Government Initiatives
- United States: $2.8B National AI Research Institutes program
- European Union: EUR 1.6B Digital Europe Programme
- China: Target of 500,000 new AI professionals by 2027
