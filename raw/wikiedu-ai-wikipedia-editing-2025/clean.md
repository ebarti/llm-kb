# Generative AI and Wikipedia Editing: What We Learned in 2025

**Author:** LiAnna Davis, Wiki Education
**Date:** January 29, 2026

## Key Findings

Wiki Education investigated generative AI usage among their program participants, discovering critical issues with AI-generated Wikipedia content.

### Detection & Scope

- Reviewed 3,078 articles created since 2022
- 178 articles (5.8%) flagged as AI-generated using Pangram detection tool
- Zero AI-detection before ChatGPT's November 2022 launch; steady increase thereafter

### Critical Problem

More than two-thirds of AI-flagged articles failed verification. "The article contained a plausible-sounding sentence, cited to a real, relevant-sounding source. But when you read the source... the information on Wikipedia does not exist" in that specific source. Only 7% contained entirely fabricated sources.

## 2025 Term Results

- 6,357 new editors supported in fall 2025
- Only 217 participants (3%) had multiple AI alerts
- 5% experienced mainspace AI alerts
- 1,406 total AI edit alerts; 314 (22%) in live articles

## Beneficial AI Applications

Survey of 102 responses showed 87% found AI helpful for:
- Identifying article gaps
- Locating reliable sources
- Evaluating drafts against requirements
- Grammar correction

## Policy & Community Response

English Wikipedia adopted a guideline against using large language models to generate new articles. Wiki Education implemented automated detection systems, training modules, and intervention protocols.

## Core Recommendation

"Wikipedia editors should never copy and paste the output from generative AI chatbots like ChatGPT into Wikipedia articles."

## Conclusion

AI tools serve research purposes when critically evaluated by humans, but autonomous text generation poses unacceptable verification risks to Wikipedia's integrity.
