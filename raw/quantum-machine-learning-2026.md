# Quantum Machine Learning (QML) in 2026

## Key Algorithms

Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs): Quantum circuits with adjustable parameters optimized using classical optimization methods, functioning similarly to neural network training.

Quantum Kernel Methods: Leverage quantum computers to evaluate similarity measures in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces that classical systems cannot efficiently compute.

Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithms (QAOA): Quantum annealing for combinatorial problems.

Quantum Sampling: Enables sampling from distributions that are classically hard to sample from.

## Hardware Platforms

- IBM Qiskit Machine Learning: Tools for building quantum neural networks
- Google TensorFlow Quantum: Hybrid quantum-classical integration
- Amazon Braket: Managed quantum computing access
- IonQ: Trapped-ion quantum computing through Braket ecosystem
- ORCA Computing: Photonic quantum systems with hybrid classical-quantum platforms

## Current Capabilities vs. Limitations

Capabilities:
- Superposition and entanglement enable parallel processing
- Potential for drug discovery and molecular simulation
- Portfolio optimization and risk assessment applications

Limitations:
- Current quantum computers suffer from noise, limited coherence times, and relatively few qubits
- Barren plateaus cause gradient vanishing during training
- Data loading bottleneck creates overhead in encoding classical data
- Demonstrated quantum advantage for practical ML tasks remains limited
- State-of-the-art machines have hundreds of qubits, but enterprise-scale AI needs thousands or millions

## 2026 Status

QML is transitioning from theoretical promise to practical applications. IBM's 2026 quantum advantage target and Google's breakthroughs signal error-corrected scalable systems are coming.

First QUB system going online at Elevate Quantum in 2026 — first commercially reproducible modular quantum computing platform.

Hybrid quantum-classical is the dominant model: quantum computers handle optimization and complex simulations while classical HPC handles general-purpose workloads.

ORCA Computing partnered with Poznan Supercomputing Center and NVIDIA to deploy hybrid quantum-classical platform using NVIDIA's CUDA-Q units.
