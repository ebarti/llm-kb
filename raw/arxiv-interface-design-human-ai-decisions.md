# Engaging with AI: How Interface Design Shapes Human-AI Collaboration in High-Stakes Decision-Making

## Abstract

Examines how interface design influences human-AI team performance in high-stakes contexts like healthcare. Addresses a critical problem: "human + AI teams have been shown to perform worse than AI alone" due to automation bias — users over-rely on AI recommendations regardless of accuracy.

## Methodology

Tested six decision-support mechanisms across two categories:
- Text and visual explanations (XAI approaches)
- Four cognitive forcing functions (CFFs)

108 participants in a diabetes management scenario.

## Key Findings

### What Worked
- AI confidence levels enhanced collaborative performance
- Text explanations improved trust and decision quality
- Performance visualizations helped users calibrate reliance
- Mechanisms providing "AI reasoning clues" improved trust

### What Backfired
- Human feedback mechanisms encouraged reflection but **reduced task performance** by increasing cognitive load
- AI-driven questions similarly reduced performance despite prompting engagement
- Higher cognitive effort subsequently **damaged trust levels**

## Core Insight

Critical design tension: mechanisms that prompt deeper engagement often create excessive cognitive burden, paradoxically harming decision outcomes. Effective interface design must "strike a balance in CFF and XAI design" rather than maximizing either explanations or forcing functions independently.

## Key Implication

Interface designers cannot assume that more engagement mechanisms automatically improve human-AI collaboration — thoughtful calibration is essential for high-stakes domains. The relationship between engagement depth and performance is an inverted U-curve, not a linear improvement.
