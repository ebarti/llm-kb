# Wisdom of the Crowd

## Definition

"The collective opinion of a diverse and independent group of individuals...yields the best judgement." Aggregated knowledge from many people often surpasses expert opinion alone.

## Historical Development

### Aristotle's Early Formulation
First to document the principle: people gathered collectively "may be better, not individually but collectively, than those who are so."

### Francis Galton (1906)
Classic example: 800 participants guessing an ox's weight at a Plymouth fair. "The median guess, 1207 pounds, was accurate within 1% of the true weight of 1198 pounds."

## Conditions for Effective Crowd Wisdom

- **Diversity**: Participants must have varied perspectives and backgrounds
- **Independence**: Individual judgments should remain uninfluenced by others
- **Decentralization**: Decision-making authority should be distributed
- **Aggregation capability**: Responses must be combinable into meaningful conclusions

## Key Mechanisms

### Diversity Prediction Theorem (Scott E. Page)
"The squared error of the collective prediction equals the average squared error minus the predictive diversity." Larger group diversity directly reduces collective error.

### Probability Distribution Model
Crowd responses modeled as samples from a probability distribution. Random errors cancel out during averaging, leaving more accurate collective estimates.

### The "Crowd Within"
Individuals may draw estimates from internal probability distributions. A person's multiple estimates, when averaged, can produce values closer to truth than single judgments.

### Surprisingly Popular Method (MIT)
Asks people both what they believe and what they think others believe. "The 'surprisingly popular' algorithm reduces errors by 21.3 percent in comparison to simple majority votes."

## Applications

- **Trial by Jury**: Practical implementation of crowd wisdom
- **Digital Platforms**: Wikipedia, Reddit, Stack Exchange leverage collective knowledge
- **Prediction Markets**: Forecasting elections and events
- **Business Intelligence**: Aggregated consumer feedback for strategy

## Critical Limitations

### Systematic Biases
"Averaging can eliminate random errors...but not systematic errors that affect the opinions of the entire crowd in the same way."

### Conditions for Failure
- Questions lacking objectively correct answers
- Communication between participants (reducing independence)
- Social influence pressures
- Unevenly distributed expertise

### Strategic Voting
"People participating in the aggregation of opinions...often do not share their sincere opinion, but engage in strategic voting."

### Digital Platform Challenges
Effectiveness compromised by "demographic biases, the influence of highly active users, and the presence of bots."

## Mitigation Strategies

- Use "geometric mean and the median" rather than simple averages (more robust to social pressure)
- WICRO algorithm "gauges the expertise level of individuals by assessing the relative 'distance' between them"
- Ensure "the group contains a population with diverse backgrounds"
- Multi-media approach to aggregate intelligence from various platforms
