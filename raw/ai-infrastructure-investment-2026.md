# AI Infrastructure Investment 2026

## Overall Scale

- Gartner: Worldwide AI spending forecast at $2.5 trillion in 2026, 44% increase over 2025
- Jensen Huang estimate: $3-4 trillion on AI infrastructure by end of decade
- Goldman Sachs consensus: $527 billion in Big Tech 2026 capex (up from $465B earlier estimates)
- Over $1 trillion in 2025 commitments across 8 major projects, including $500B Stargate Initiative

## Company-by-Company 2026 Capex

- Amazon: $200 billion projected (up from $131B in 2025) — capex leader
- Google/Alphabet: $175-185 billion (up from $91B in 2025)
- Meta: $115-135 billion — most aggressive buildout in company history; $600B US infrastructure commitment through 2028
- Microsoft: Major nuclear commitments (2 GW with Constellation Energy through 2040)

## What the Money Buys

- Data center construction and land
- GPU/accelerator procurement (NVIDIA Blackwell primary)
- Power infrastructure and generation
- Cooling systems (liquid cooling reaching 47% adoption by 2026)
- Networking equipment (InfiniBand, optical interconnects)

## Market Dynamics

- AI server shipments growing over 20% year-over-year in 2026
- Inference projected at two-thirds of total AI compute spending by 2026 (up from one-third in 2023)
- Specialized inference ASICs could capture 45% of inference market by 2030
- Foundational AI startups raised $178B in Q1 2026 alone (vs $88.9B all of 2025)

## Key Deals

- OpenAI-Cerebras: $10B+ contract for 750 MW of computing power through 2028
- Stargate Initiative: $500B joint venture
- Apple-Broadcom: Baltra AI inference chip for internal use (2026)

## Federal Reserve Analysis

The Fed published analysis of "Global Trade Effects of the AI Infrastructure Boom" in February 2026, indicating the investment wave is reshaping international trade flows.

## Market Risks

- Potential for overinvestment if AI revenue growth doesn't match capex
- Power grid constraints limiting data center expansion
- Export controls creating geographic imbalances
- Competition from custom silicon reducing NVIDIA's pricing power
