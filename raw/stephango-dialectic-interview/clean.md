# Steph Ango on Obsidian, Tools, and Design Philosophy

## Core Interview Insights

Podcast conversation between Jackson Dahl and Steph Ango (CEO of Obsidian) explores how constraints drive creativity, the philosophy behind building sustainable software, and what makes for genuinely thoughtful design.

### Obsidian's Principled Approach

Unlike venture-backed competitors, Obsidian operates with five core manifesto principles:
1. Independence
2. User-only funding (no investors)
3. Small team size (7-12 people)
4. Privacy
5. Data durability

Ango emphasizes that "the company is secondary to my personal goals of using the tool I want." This inverted priority — building for himself first, others second — paradoxically creates better products because he remains a power user testing daily.

The "file over app" philosophy represents a civilizational stance: data should live in open formats users control, not proprietary cloud silos. Ango notes that "plain text formats maximize the chance your data survives a thousand years."

### On Constraints as Creative Force

Ango argues that constraints paradoxically enable freedom. He traces this to early experiences designing Winamp skins within strict pixel limitations. "Self-imposed constraints become your signature" through consistent application across mediums.

### Design Philosophy: Care Over Scale

Ango's design approach centers on one principle: "The recipe for success is caring more than someone else." Rather than chasing growth metrics, he prioritizes craftsmanship details others overlook — "painting the back of the cabinet you'll never see."

### Tools and Agency

A thinking tool's value lies in expanding human capability. Obsidian succeeds because linking ideas mimics how minds naturally work. The plugin architecture lets millions customize the tool without the team building everything — tools should conform to users rather than vice versa.

### On Permanence vs. Performance

Ango acknowledges tension between Obsidian's sustainability mission and shipping speed. His solution involves "zigzagging" — alternating between maximalist exploration and minimalist shipping deadlines. This prevents over-attachment to features and clarifies what's truly necessary.

### The Stadium of Selves Metaphor

Ango's writing strategy imagines his past and future selves as an audience. His five-year rule for recommending products reflects patience with verification.
