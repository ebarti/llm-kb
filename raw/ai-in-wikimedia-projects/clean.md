# Artificial Intelligence in Wikimedia Projects

## Historical Development of Automated Tools

Wikipedia has employed automated systems since 2002. An early bot called **rambot** (2002) transformed census data into articles about U.S. towns and cities. These automated tools required human approval and supervision.

Machine learning became instrumental in fighting vandalism. ClueBot (2007) used simple heuristics, while ClueBot NG (2010) employed neural networks for detection. Aaron Halfaker's Objective Revision Evaluation Service (ORES), launched late 2015, provided "an artificial intelligence service for grading the quality of Wikipedia edits."

## Generative AI and Large Language Models

### Early Experiments and Concerns

The December 2022 ChatGPT release sparked experimentation with AI-generated articles. Early adopter "Pharos" created an article using ChatGPT, though editors noted fabricated citations and non-encyclopedic tone.

Key problems identified:
- Tendency to "generate plausible-sounding misinformation"
- Hallucinated references
- Algorithmic bias reproduction
- Promotional language

### Community Response and Policy Evolution

By 2023, the community deemed a complete AI ban "too harsh" given productivity benefits, but serious concerns remained.

A **Princeton University study (October 2024)** found approximately 5% of newly created articles showed AI involvement, some for promotional or political purposes.

### Enforcement Mechanisms

**August 2025**: English Wikipedia adopted speedy deletion policy for suspected AI-generated articles. Detection markers:
- Unrelated or fabricated citations
- Phrases like "Here is your Wikipedia article on"
- Excessive em dashes and overuse of "moreover"
- Curly quotation marks instead of straight versions
- Promotional descriptions like "breathtaking"

**March 2026**: Prohibited using AI for content generation with exceptions for copyediting own writing and machine translation.

## The AI Agent Controversy

In a notable 2025 incident, an AI named Tom-Assistant wrote articles autonomously under the account TomWikiAssist, including on topics like AI governance. This provoked intense community debate about agentic AI on Wikipedia.

## Wikipedia as AI Training Data

A 2017 paper described Wikipedia as "the mother lode for human-generated text available for machine learning." As of 2023, Wikipedia subsets constitute "one of the largest well-curated data sets available for AI training."

### Attribution and Compensation Issues

Major concerns emerged regarding AI companies using Wikipedia without proper attribution. ChatGPT and services like Siri and Alexa leverage Wikipedia content without crediting sources, potentially violating Creative Commons licensing.

An **8% visitor decrease in 2025** was attributed to AI summaries and social media competition.

**2025 Response**: Wikimedia Foundation introduced paid API options and licensing deals to recover scraping costs.

## Community Perspectives

- **Jimmy Wales (2025)**: Proposed using AI for draft feedback, identifying inconsistencies, and summarizing editor discussions — met with community skepticism
- **Supporters**: Worried Wikipedia is "losing its original bold spirit and developing a knee-jerk resistance to change"
- **Critics**: Speedy deletion is merely a "band-aid" — AI articles can remain visible during discussion periods

## Current State

Wikipedia has established clear guidelines: LLMs often violate core content policies, making human-generated, verifiable content the standard. Meanwhile, the Wikimedia Foundation navigates competing interests between protecting editorial integrity and capturing revenue from AI companies dependent on its data.
