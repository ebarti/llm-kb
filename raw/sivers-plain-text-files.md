# Write Plain Text Files — Derek Sivers

## Core Arguments for Plain Text

**Portability**: Sivers emphasizes that plain text works across all devices and operating systems. He notes, "Every device, including ones long gone, and ones not invented yet, can read and edit plain text." This has been his practice since 1990, moving through various platforms and devices.

**Independence from Commercial Tools**: The author warns against vendor lock-in, stating that relying on proprietary formats means "you need that program to access it." He argues that "You will outlive these companies. Your writing should outlive you."

**Offline Capability**: Plain text enables work without internet access, providing focus benefits during offline periods.

**No Technical Dependencies**: Rather than accumulating tools, Sivers maintains that "Plain text files and a basic text editor are enough" for effective thinking and writing.

**Conversion Flexibility**: Plain text converts easily into HTML, Markdown, LaTeX, and other formats. He uses this approach for his four books and four hundred blog posts.

## Personal Practices

Sivers views his writing as "extended memory" and "a history of my life." He organizes files hierarchically using folders and demonstrates metadata organization through date and tag fields in diary entries. Since 1990, he has written, blogged, journaled, and coded in plain text.

## Key Philosophy

He emphasizes the "tranquility" of plain text's offline, non-commercial nature, describing them as "quiet" and "focused" — qualities he personally values. The approach prioritizes longevity and independence over features and convenience.
