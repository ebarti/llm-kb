# AI Energy Demand — IEA Analysis

## Current Consumption (2024)

- Data center electricity: ~415 TWh (~1.5% of global electricity consumption)
- Annual growth rate: 12% over the last five years

## 2030 Projections (Base Case)

Global data center electricity consumption will double:
- Target: ~945 TWh by 2030
- Share of global consumption: Just under 3%
- Annual growth rate: ~15% (2024-2030) — four times faster than overall electricity demand growth

By equipment type:
- Accelerated servers (AI-driven): 30% annual growth
- Conventional servers: 9% annual growth
- Accelerated servers account for ~50% of net increase
- Cooling and infrastructure: ~20% of net increase

## Regional Distribution (2030)

- United States: +240 TWh (130% increase)
- China: +175 TWh (170% increase)
- Europe: +45 TWh (70% increase)
- Per capita (US): Projected to reach 1,200 kWh per capita — roughly 10% of annual household electricity

## 2026 Milestones

- IEA projects global data center electricity consumption hits 1,100 TWh in 2026, equivalent to Japan's entire national consumption
- 18% upward revision from December 2025 estimates
- Inference now dominant energy consumer, surpassing model training at fleet scale
- Virginia grid operators issued formal capacity warnings through 2028
- Northern Virginia halted new data center permits until power infrastructure catches up

## Sensitivity Scenarios

- Lift-Off Case: 1,700+ TWh by 2035 (4.4% of global demand)
- High Efficiency Case: 970 TWh by 2035 with 15%+ energy savings
- Headwinds Case: ~700 TWh (less than 2% of global demand)

## Industry Response

- Microsoft: 2 GW nuclear commitment with Constellation Energy through 2040
- Amazon: 1.5 GW dedicated solar in Texas
- Google: Liquid cooling breakthroughs reducing power overhead by 30% in TPU v6 clusters
- TDP per chip: jumping from 700W (H100/H200) to over 1,000W (B200/B300)
- Liquid-cooling adoption expected to reach 47% of server racks by 2026

## Broader Context

Data center growth accounts for less than 10% of total global electricity demand increase (2024-2030), despite concentration in specific geographic locations creating grid integration challenges.
