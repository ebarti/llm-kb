# Neuralink BCI 2026: Medical Device vs Transhumanism

## Current Status (January 2026)

- 12 people worldwide with severe paralysis have received Neuralink implants
- Patients include those with ALS and quadriplegia from spinal cord injuries
- All recipients now use BCIs to communicate, type, and control digital tools using thought alone
- The Link device: 23mm diameter, 8mm thick (size of a quarter)

## High-Volume Production Plans

Neuralink announced plans to begin "high-volume production" of BCI devices in 2026, moving to a streamlined, almost entirely automated surgical procedure.

## Core Tension: Medical vs Transhumanist

The article highlights a fundamental tension:
- **Medical device path**: Focus on helping people with ALS, quadriplegia, and other severe conditions regain computer control
- **Transhumanist rhetoric**: Executives publicly discuss "machine-human symbiosis" and consumer applications
- Competitors worry this messaging hampers "the ability of startups developing brain-computer interfaces to gain approval as medical devices and be paid for by health insurers"

## Regulatory Concerns

Neuralink recruited a senior FDA official who had been overseeing the company's regulation — described as simultaneously impressive and troubling to competitors concerned about undue influence.

## International Competition

China approved its first invasive BCI product in March 2026 — Neuracle Technology (Shanghai) received marketing approval for a brain implant for adult patients partially paralyzed from spinal cord injury.

## Clinical Applications Beyond Paralysis

The technology holds promise for:
- Neurodegenerative diseases
- Mental health disorders: depression, anxiety, PTSD
- Potential future: memory enhancement, cognitive augmentation

## The Link Technology

- Reads electrical signals from neurons
- Translates neural signals into digital commands
- Enables cursor control, typing, and device interaction through thought
- Minimally invasive implantation procedure (increasingly automated)
