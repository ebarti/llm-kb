# Copyright and AI Training Data (2025-2026)

## Copyright Office Guidance (May 2025)

108-page report concluded "some uses of copyrighted works for generative AI training will qualify as fair use, and some will not." Fair use determinations are highly fact-specific.

## Key 2025 Court Decisions

### Bartz v. Anthropic & Kadrey v. Meta (June 2025)
Northern District of California ruled (two days apart) that using copyrighted works to train AI models was "highly transformative" and fair use. Both rulings narrow and fact-specific.

### Thomson Reuters v. ROSS Intelligence (February 2025)
District of Delaware granted partial summary judgment to Thomson Reuters, holding that headnotes were protectable and ROSS's fair-use defense failed.

## Emerging Judicial Consensus

A consensus is developing that training a general-purpose AI model is "highly transformative" — a factor favoring fair use. But significant disagreements persist between courts.

## 2026 Outlook

- Cases involving OpenAI and Google expected
- Total number of AI copyright cases may peak in 2026
- Expected: sharper challenges to fair-use defenses, aggressive discovery into proprietary training data, new wave of class certification battles
- Litigation focus shifting from training data to AI outputs

## Key Tension

Balance between protecting creative rights and enabling AI development. No final answers expected in 2026.
