# Photonic Computing for AI

## How It Works

Photonic chips use light particles (photons) instead of electrons to process and transmit data. The technology manipulates light waves through integrated optical components including lasers, modulators, and detectors on a single chip, enabling data to travel at the speed of light.

## Performance Advantages

- Speed: Optical signals travel at approximately 300 million meters per second, reducing latency significantly compared to electrical signals
- Energy efficiency: Photonic chips consume substantially less power than electronic equivalents, particularly for long-distance data transmission, since optical signals don't suffer from resistive heat losses
- Interference immunity: Unlike electronic circuits affected by electromagnetic interference, optical signals remain unaffected by environmental factors

## Manufacturing Platforms

Three dominant platforms exist:
- Indium phosphide (InP) for high-performance applications
- Silicon nitride (SiN) for sensing and telecommunications
- Silicon photonics (SiPh) for data communication

## Current Limitations

Major barriers to adoption include:
- Manufacturing complexity requiring nanometre-scale precision in specialized clean facilities
- Integration challenges combining photonic and electronic components
- Limited talent and infrastructure compared to traditional semiconductor expertise
- Lack of industry standardization, forcing proprietary solutions

## Timeline for Commercialization

Complete revolution by 2026 appears unrealistic. Significant adoption in specific applications projected. Conservative estimates: approximately 300 million units by 2030 and potentially 1 billion by 2040.

## Key Developments (2024-2026)

- LightGen: All-optical chip for vision generation with 100x speed and energy efficiency over electronic chips (Science, 2026)
- MIT photonic processor: DNN computations in less than half a nanosecond with 92%+ accuracy
- OFC 2026: Co-packaged optics (CPOs) transition from pluggable optics — major AI networking trend
- Neurophos: Commercial photonic processor running ResNet, BERT, and Atari RL algorithms
- Lightmatter: Photonic interconnect and compute company; "photonic supercomputer" vision

## AI Applications

Photonic chips enable parallel processing capabilities and massive computational throughput for ML training and scientific simulation. Near-term focus is on data center interconnects (replacing electrical with optical links between GPU clusters) rather than compute itself.
