# Federated Wiki

## Creator and Origins

Ward Cunningham developed Federated Wiki (formerly Smallest Federated Wiki), launching at IndieWebCamp 2011. Initial release: December 16, 2013. Current stable release: 0.38.5 (October 19, 2025).

## Core Concept

Rather than maintaining a single canonical version of content like traditional wikis, Federated Wiki enables users to "fork wiki pages, maintaining their own copies." This architecture supports what Cunningham describes as "a chorus of voices" where participants share information while preserving individual viewpoints.

## How It Differs from Traditional Wikis

Contrasts sharply with Wikipedia and similar systems that rely on consensus-based management of single articles per topic. Federated Wiki embraces "a multi-point of view approach," allowing diverse perspectives to coexist rather than be consolidated into one authoritative version.

## Key Design Principles

- **Forking**: Users can fork any page to their own wiki, modify it, and keep their version
- **Federation**: Individual wiki instances communicate and share content across a network
- **Chorus of voices**: Multiple versions of the same topic can coexist, each reflecting a different perspective
- **No single authority**: No central editorial control; authority is distributed

## Relationship to Wikimedia Federation

The Wikimedia ecosystem has a parallel concept: federated knowledge graphs using Wikibase. Federation in Wikibase means sharing information between instances to broaden the base of available data and prevent siloization. The Wikimedia vision includes three layers:
- **Underlays**: Grounding layers of observations and primary source data
- **Overlays**: Experiential lenses for understanding subsets of information
- **Interlace**: The connective matrix of ideas, discoveries, and knowledge projects

## Significance

Federated Wiki represents a fundamentally different approach to collaborative knowledge: instead of converging on one "truth," it preserves the plurality of perspectives while enabling cross-pollination between individual knowledge spaces.
