# Cyc: History's Forgotten AI Project

## The Visionary: Doug Lenat
Stanford professor who achieved earlier success with:
- AM (Automated Mathematician): Discovered mathematical theorems, some mirroring work by Ramanujan
- EURISKO: Modified its own heuristics, won a board game tournament through unorthodox strategy

## Project Genesis
In 1983, Lenat convened AI researchers at Stanford. They calculated genuine machine intelligence required several million rules — approximately 2,000 person-years of effort.

MCC formed in 1984 to counter Japanese fifth-generation computing. Lenat accepted chief scientist position with "a half-billion dollar budget and hundreds of employees."

## Technical Architecture
- 1.5 million concepts, 25 million rules
- Over 1,000 specialized inference engines
- Frames (units) with slots containing property values
- Global ontology with "ontological engineers" manually analyzing English text
- Microtheories reconciling contradictory facts across domains
- Default reasoning distinguishing estimates from absolute facts

## Commercial Phase (1994-)
MCC disbanded; Cyc became Cycorp. Notable applications:
- Cleveland Clinic: Reduced medical research query time from months to under an hour
- U.S. intelligence agencies: Terrorism knowledge databases

## The Decline
Field shifted toward deep learning (~2012). Machine learning proved more successful at image recognition, game-playing, and language understanding.

Failed prediction (1989, Lenat & Guha): "By 1999 no one would even think about having a computer that doesn't have Cyc running on it."

## Legacy
Now employs 50 staff. Largely remembered as "a cautionary tale of tremendous effort wasted on a misguided approach."

Lenat's final paper (before 2023 death) proposed complementary synergies:
- LLMs: fluency and breadth but lack consistency
- Cyc: auditable reasoning chains without natural language facility
- Integrated system might combine both strengths

## Significance for Modern AI
Cyc represents the most thorough attempt to hand-encode human knowledge. Its failure to achieve goals, alongside the success of neural approaches that learn representations from data, is one of the defining stories of AI's shift from symbolic to connectionist paradigms.
