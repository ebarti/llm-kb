# AI Is Changing the Physics of Collective Intelligence — How Do We Respond?

**Authors:** Jacob Taylor and Scott E. Page
**Publication:** Brookings Institution, December 16, 2025

## Main Arguments

The authors argue that generative AI is fundamentally altering "how ideas, data, and decisions flow through groups and institutions." Rather than simply adopting AI as an add-on tool, organizations must redesign collaboration systems to harness AI's potential for strengthening shared problem-solving.

## Two Distinct Approaches to Policymaking

The authors identify a historical divide between two groups:

1. **Design-minded practitioners** (psychologists, anthropologists, sociologists) who organize convenings and shape collaborative processes
2. **Model-minded researchers** (economists, epidemiologists, systems scientists) who build simulations and causal frameworks

While both address complex policy coordination, they operate in parallel with "distinct cultures and languages."

## The Fundamental Problem

Current approaches have limitations. Design-focused rooms can only accommodate limited perspectives and timeframes, often reaching "local minimum" solutions. Conversely, models capture system-wide dynamics but lack grounding in actual on-the-ground implementation details and real-time community insights.

## How AI Changes the Physics

The authors propose AI as a **translation engine** bridging these camps:

- AI can capture and structure real-time deliberation from collaborative processes, making tacit knowledge explicit
- Large language models can connect deliberation data to simulations and models
- This enables feedback loops where rooms inform models, and models provide decision support back to collaborative groups

The authors reference their "vibe teaming" experiments, where AI functions as an integrated teammate rather than a peripheral tool, "scaffold[ing] better human performance instead of crowding it out."

## Practical Example: Green Energy Transition

Consider government decisions on accelerating renewable energy. Design-focused groups develop narratives about implementation ("who needs to do what by when"), while modeling teams produce probability distributions of outcomes across communities. AI could integrate these complementary perspectives into coherent policy guidance.

## Four Core Research Questions

1. When does AI in collaborative settings enhance understanding versus distraction?
2. How can room outputs update models to reflect ground realities rather than formal strategies?
3. What infrastructure (digital identities, registries, protocols, governance) enables trust in shared tools?
4. How do we cultivate collaborative cultures where these processes excel?

## Proposed Demonstration

The authors suggest coupling existing collaborative formats (like 17 Rooms initiatives for Sustainable Development Goals) with system-dynamics modeling platforms used by governments or multilateral institutions. Initial data flows would track which interventions are being attempted, which coalitions form, and what succeeds locally — feeding this into updated models that inform subsequent collaborative sessions.

## Conclusion

The authors contend that deliberate design of "room+model" systems enhanced by AI could drive more effective, inclusive, and adaptive problem-solving across policy domains. Success requires collaboration among "collaboration nerds, complex systems nerds, and AI nerds" to establish new norms and processes that strengthen rather than undermine collective intelligence.
