# Luhmann's Original Zettelkasten Method

## Two Slip Box Systems

Luhmann maintained two independent systems, not one:

1. **Bibliographic Slip-Box**: A reference index containing complete publication details and page references. Front side held citation information; back side listed page numbers with relevant concepts. This was purely an indexing tool for locating sources.

2. **Main Slip-Box**: The core thinking tool containing only ideas Luhmann had personally processed and reformulated. Cards here were written in his own language (not excerpts), independently understandable, connectable with other ideas, and units of single concepts.

The systems remained largely separate — one recorded sources, the other developed ideas.

## The Workflow: Separating Reading from Reaction

Luhmann's process had three distinct stages:

1. **While Reading**: Record bibliographic information on cards without personal commentary
2. **Daily Reflection**: Re-read cards and consider their relevance to current research
3. **Rewriting**: Transform useful ideas into standalone cards in own language

Critically, Luhmann did not highlight in books. He believed this was merely "marking others' ideas." True thinking occurred during the rewriting phase.

## Folgezettel: The Numbering System

The numbering system used organic branching rather than traditional hierarchy:
- Initial card: 1
- Continuation: 1a, 1b, 1c
- Nested expansion: 1a1, 1a1a, 1a1b

This created tree-like structures but remained flexible — new cards could be inserted at any level without reorganizing existing ones. The numbering captured the evolutionary path of thinking, not topic classification.

## Fixed Position Ordering (Feste Stellordnung)

Luhmann explicitly rejected thematic classification. Instead, he used fixed position ordering — each card maintained its assigned number permanently. Benefits:
- Preventing unexpected connections from being limited by topic boxes
- Allowing single ideas to exist in multiple contexts
- Creating "organized chaos" that sparked creative discoveries
- Enabling cards to have meaning through network position rather than classification

## Hub Notes

Hub Notes were special cards containing extensive reference lists — essentially "highways" between topics, helping navigation through 90,000 cards.

## Communication Partner Concept

Luhmann described the system as a "communication partner" — an external mind capable of dialogue. As it grew, the system would surface forgotten ideas, reveal unexpected connections, challenge and inspire thinking, and never feel "finished."

He stated: "The slip box can be viewed as a communication partner... As the system grows, it becomes a true interlocutor."

## Two Complete Systems in One Career

- **Zettelkasten I** (1950s–1963): Law/administration studies; ~23,000 main cards, 1,800 bibliographic entries
- **Zettelkasten II** (1963–1998): Sociology research; ~66,000 main cards, 16,000 bibliographic entries

He rebuilt completely when shifting research fields rather than expanding the existing system.

## Key Principle

"Without writing, one cannot think; at least not in a sophisticated, connectable manner." The physical act of reformulating ideas in writing constituted thinking itself.

## Modern vs. Original

Important distinction: Soenke Ahrens' 2017 "How to Take Smart Notes" introduced the three-part classification of "fleeting notes," "literature notes," and "permanent notes." This terminology was not Luhmann's original framework — it represents later systematization, though based on his actual practice. Luhmann's actual structure was simpler: two slip boxes, with a workflow separating the reading stage from the thinking stage.
