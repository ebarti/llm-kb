# Generative AI and the Digital Commons

**Authors:** Saffron Huang and Divya Siddarth
**Organization:** Collective Intelligence Project

## Core Argument

Generative foundation models (GFMs) — systems like GPT-3, ChatGPT, DALL-E, and Stable Diffusion — simultaneously depend on and threaten the digital commons.

### The Dependency

GFMs rely heavily on publicly available data: "Generative foundation models leverage large databases of scraped information... including painstakingly edited Wikipedia, millennia's worth of books, billions of Reddit comments, hundreds of terabytes' worth of images." Yet companies rarely compensate contributors or return value to these commons.

## Seven Key Risks

1. **Information Pollution**: GFMs generate low-quality, biased, or false content faster than humans can verify it, degrading shared knowledge resources.
2. **Democratic Erosion**: Models enable personalized disinformation and manipulation, with design decisions by small teams lacking diverse input.
3. **Content Homogenization**: Reliance on similar models produces increasingly uniform online information.
4. **Reduced Commons Contributions**: People may stop creating original content for shared platforms, fearing labor displacement or unwanted training data use.
5. **Economic Concentration**: Proprietary model control by few companies could mirror historical infrastructure monopolies.
6. **Labor Precarity**: Automation threatens jobs in copywriting, digital art, and similar fields without adequate worker protections.
7. **Unpredictable AI Risks**: Highly capable black-box systems pose emerging dangers.

## Governance Proposals

### Proposal I: Monitoring & Standards Consortia

Multi-stakeholder organizations should establish transparency standards, audit frameworks, and develop shared tools for detecting AI-generated content. Requires diverse representation to prevent incumbent capture.

### Proposal II: Commons Data Contributions

GFM companies should contribute high-quality datasets to public repositories, enabling researchers to track labor impacts and deployment effects while improving transparency around training data.

### Proposal III: Data-Based Governance Structures

Communities and individuals providing training data could receive ownership stakes or governance rights:
- Users earning credits for feedback improving models
- Artists collectively training image models they govern
- Privacy-preserving data intermediaries for sensitive domains (healthcare, finance)

## Critical Context

"Data lends itself especially well to a commons framework" because both inputs and impacts are fundamentally shared. Without intervention, the digital commons faces a paradox: it enables GFM development while GFM outputs potentially degrade the very resources these systems depend upon.
