# File Over App — Steph Ango

## Core Principle

"File over app is a philosophy: if you want to create digital artifacts that last, they must be files you can control, in formats that are easy to retrieve and read." This concept was articulated by Steph Ango, the CEO of Obsidian.

## Key Arguments

**Files Outlast Applications**: Ango emphasizes that apps inevitably become obsolete, but the files created within them can persist across centuries. The content matters more than the tool — much like ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs outlasted the chisels used to carve them.

**Historical Precedent**: Throughout history, human ideas survived through durable mediums: clay tablets, manuscripts, paintings, and tapestries. These tangible artifacts required minimal technology to access and preserve. Today's cloud-dependent files lack this permanence.

**Control and Accessibility**: Most digital content remains trapped in proprietary formats, server databases, and locked ecosystems. "If you want your writing readable on computers from the 2060s or 2160s, it's important that your notes can be read on computers from the 1960s," Ango notes — advocating for backwards compatibility and independence from commercial platforms.

**Personal Relevance**: Beyond posterity, durability matters for your future self. Accessing old work becomes impossible when formats become incompatible or services disappear.

## Call to Action

The philosophy is "an appeal to tool makers to accept that all software is ephemeral, and in the fullness of time, the files you create are more important than the tools you use to create them." Ango encourages both users to choose file-based tools and developers to prioritize user ownership over proprietary systems.

## Connection to Obsidian

Obsidian embodies this philosophy by storing everything as plain markdown files on the user's device. No cloud dependency, no proprietary database — just files that any text editor can read.
