## Overview

Helix is [[entities/figure-ai]]'s proprietary [[concepts/vision-language-action-models]] system, developed after ending the company's partnership with OpenAI in 2025. Brett Adcock explained the strategic pivot: "large language models had become a smaller problem compared to those allowing for 'high rate robot control.'" Helix focuses on the millisecond-level motor commands needed for [[concepts/dexterous-manipulation]].

## Key Facts

- **Type**: framework (proprietary VLA)
- **Organization**: [[entities/figure-ai]]
- **Versions**: Helix 01-02, Helix 02 (full-body, Jan 2026)
- **Notable for**: Full-body humanoid autonomy; multi-robot simultaneous control

## Versions

- **Helix 01-02**: Controls up to 2 robots simultaneously. Enables interaction "without extensive manual training." Figure 02 can "pick up nearly any small household object."
- **Helix 02** (January 2026): Full-body autonomy expanding AI control across entire robot body. Demonstrated loading/unloading dishwashers using motion-capture data + simulation-based ML.

## Significance

Helix represents a strategic bet that the bottleneck for [[concepts/humanoid-robots]] is not language understanding (solved-enough by LLMs) but high-frequency motor control. The shift from OpenAI's language models to proprietary control systems signals a maturation in the field's understanding of where the hardest problems lie.
