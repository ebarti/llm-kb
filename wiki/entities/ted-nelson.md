## Overview

Theodor Holm "Ted" Nelson (born 1937) is an American pioneer of information technology who coined the terms "hypertext" and "hypermedia" in 1965. He founded Project Xanadu in 1960, the first hypertext project, which envisioned a global system of interconnected documents with bidirectional links, [[concepts/transclusion]], version tracking, and built-in micropayments.

## Key Contributions

### Coining "Hypertext" (1965)
At the 1965 ACM National Conference, Nelson presented "Complex Information Processing: A File Structure for the Complex, the Changing, and the Indeterminate," introducing "zippered lists" and coining "[[concepts/hypertext]]."

### Project Xanadu (1960-present)
The first hypertext project, featuring:
- **Bidirectional links**: Visible and navigable from both endpoints (unlike the web's one-way links)
- **[[concepts/transclusion]]**: Including content from other documents by reference, not copy
- **Version tracking**: Full revision history of all documents
- **Micropayments**: Built-in royalty system for content creators
- **17 Original Rules**: A complete specification for a global knowledge operating system

### Publications
- *Computer Lib/Dream Machines* (1974): Manifesto for personal computing and hypertext
- *Literary Machines* (1981): Technical vision for Xanadu

## Controversy and Legacy
Wired magazine (1995) called Xanadu "the longest-running vaporware in computing history." The web's success with simpler, one-directional links seemed to vindicate pragmatism over Nelson's idealism.

Yet many of Nelson's "unrealized" concepts have resurfaced: Obsidian's backlinks provide bidirectional linking, wiki templates implement crude transclusion, and content provenance is now a hot topic in the LLM era. Werner Herzog's 2016 documentary featured Nelson, noting he appeared "clinically sane" in his persistence.
