## Overview

John McCarthy (1927-2011) was an American computer scientist who coined the term "artificial intelligence" for the 1956 Dartmouth Conference, invented the LISP programming language (1958), and championed formal logic as the foundation for AI. He co-founded MIT's AI Laboratory with [[entities/marvin-minsky]] and later established Stanford's AI Lab.

## Key Contributions

### Coined "Artificial Intelligence" (1955/1956)
Proposed the term for the 1956 Dartmouth Conference, the event that launched AI as a field.

### LISP (1958)
Invented the programming language that dominated American AI for decades. LISP pioneered garbage collection, dynamic typing, higher-order functions, recursion, and self-hosting compilers. It was the standard language for [[concepts/expert-systems]] and [[concepts/knowledge-representation]] research.

### The Advice Taker (1959)
Proposed using predicate calculus for common-sense reasoning — the first formal proposal for logic-based [[concepts/knowledge-representation]]. This established the "neat" tradition in AI (formal logic, mathematical rigor) in contrast to Minsky's "scruffy" approach.

### Situation Calculus
Developed with Pat Hayes, the situation calculus provided a formal framework for representing causal knowledge — how actions change the state of the world.

### Time-Sharing
Proposed the concept of computer time-sharing (multiple users sharing one computer), which was implemented and became fundamental to modern computing.
