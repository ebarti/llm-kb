## Overview

Roam Research is a note-taking tool launched in 2020 that triggered the networked note-taking revolution by bringing bidirectional linking to mainstream personal knowledge management. Self-described as "a note taking tool for networked thought" and a "graph database" for ideas, Roam demonstrated that treating every paragraph as a referenceable, linkable node could fundamentally change how people organize and connect their thinking.

## Key Features

- **Bidirectional Linking**: When you link from Note A to Note B, Note B automatically shows the backlink — creating two-way connections that mirror associative thinking
- **Block-Level References**: Every paragraph, bullet point, or sentence is an individual block that can be referenced, embedded, and linked independently
- **Daily Notes**: Default entry point is today's date, encouraging regular capture
- **Graph Database**: The underlying data model treats all content as nodes in a graph
- **Real-time Collaboration**: Shared graphs with team members

## Historical Impact

Roam's 2020 launch catalyzed the entire modern PKM tool ecosystem. Before Roam, note-taking tools organized content in folders and files. Roam demonstrated that [[concepts/networked-thought]] was not just a theoretical ideal but a practical design pattern. This directly influenced:

- [[entities/obsidian]]: Added bidirectional linking and graph visualization
- [[entities/logseq]]: Built as an open-source alternative with similar block-based architecture
- The broader "tools for thought" movement that attracted significant venture capital

## Limitations

Roam's cloud-based architecture and subscription pricing ($15/month or $165/year) created openings for local-first alternatives. Its closed-source nature and data portability concerns drove some users to Obsidian and Logseq.
