## Overview

Marvin Lee Minsky (1927-2016) was an American cognitive and computer scientist, co-founder of MIT's AI Laboratory, and one of the most influential figures in AI history. His work spans [[concepts/knowledge-representation]], neural networks, robotics, and the philosophy of mind.

## Key Contributions

### Frame Theory (mid-1970s)
Minsky developed frames as a [[concepts/knowledge-representation]] formalism: structured descriptions of stereotypical situations with slots for properties, default values, and inheritance hierarchies. Example: a "room" frame has slots for walls, floor, ceiling, with defaults that can be overridden. Frames became the basis for object-oriented programming and modern ontology languages.

### Perceptrons (1969)
Co-authored with Seymour Papert, this book demonstrated mathematical limitations of single-layer neural networks (perceptrons). The critique is widely cited as triggering the first "AI winter" for connectionist research, though Minsky and Papert's actual claims were more nuanced than the popular narrative suggests.

### Society of Mind (1986)
Proposed that intelligence emerges from the interaction of many simple, mindless agents — a theory that anticipates modern [[concepts/multi-agent-systems]] in AI.

### MIT AI Laboratory
Co-founded with [[entities/john-mccarthy]] in 1959. Led the "scruffy" approach to AI (pragmatic, engineering-focused) in contrast to McCarthy's "neat" approach (formal logic).
