## Overview

David John Chalmers is an Australian philosopher and cognitive scientist, professor at New York University, known for formulating the "hard problem of consciousness" — the question of why and how physical processes give rise to subjective experience. In the AI context, he is a co-author of the influential Butlin-Long framework for assessing [[concepts/ai-consciousness]].

## Key Facts

- **Type**: person
- **Affiliation**: New York University
- **Notable for**: The "hard problem of consciousness," co-authoring the AI consciousness indicator framework
- **Philosophical position**: Takes [[concepts/computational-functionalism]] seriously — consciousness may not require biology

## Key Contributions

1. **The Hard Problem**: Distinguished between "easy problems" of consciousness (explaining cognitive functions) and the "hard problem" (why there is subjective experience at all)
2. **Butlin-Long Framework**: Co-authored with Patrick Butlin, Robert Long, and Yoshua Bengio — proposes 14 theory-derived indicators from neuroscientific theories to assess AI consciousness
3. **Philosophical zombies**: Thought experiment arguing that consciousness is not reducible to physical function
4. **"Reality+: Virtual Worlds and the Problems of Philosophy"** (2022): Extended philosophical analysis to virtual reality and digital minds
