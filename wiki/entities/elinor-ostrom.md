## Overview

Elinor Ostrom (1933-2012) was an American political economist who won the Nobel Prize in Economics in 2009 for her analysis of economic governance, especially the commons. Her work demonstrated that communities can successfully govern shared resources without either privatization or state control, through self-organized institutional arrangements.

## Key Contributions

### Commons Governance Framework
Ostrom's eight design principles for sustainable commons governance have been adapted to digital knowledge commons by Charlotte Hess and others. The principles include clear boundaries, proportional rules, collective choice, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution, minimal state interference, and nested enterprises.

### Knowledge Commons (with Charlotte Hess)
Hess and Ostrom adapted the commons framework to knowledge resources, distinguishing "information" from "knowledge" and emphasizing that knowledge cannot become shared without "a complex set of institutions and practices." This work underpins modern understanding of [[concepts/knowledge-commons]] and [[concepts/digital-commons-governance]].

## Relevance to AI

Ostrom's framework is increasingly applied to AI governance questions: how should shared AI training data be governed? How can communities maintain control over knowledge resources when AI companies extract value at scale? The [[entities/collective-intelligence-project]]'s governance proposals draw implicitly on Ostrom's institutional design principles.
