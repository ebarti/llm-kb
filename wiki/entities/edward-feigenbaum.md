## Overview

Edward Albert Feigenbaum (born January 20, 1936) is an American computer scientist, Turing Award laureate (1994), and professor at Stanford University. He is widely known as the "father of expert systems" for his pioneering work on knowledge-based AI systems.

## Key Contributions

### Stanford Heuristic Programming Project (~1965)
Led the research group that formally introduced [[concepts/expert-systems]]. Collaborated with Bruce Buchanan and Randall Davis.

### DENDRAL
Co-created one of the first expert systems, which identified organic molecular structures from mass spectrometry data. DENDRAL proved that AI could match human expert performance in narrow domains.

### The Knowledge Principle
Feigenbaum's foundational insight: "Intelligent systems derive their power from the knowledge they possess rather than from the specific formalisms and inference schemes they use." This "knowledge is power" principle drove the entire expert systems movement and remains relevant in the LLM era, where [[concepts/data-quality-bottleneck]] confirms that data quality matters more than model sophistication.

### Turing Award (1994)
Shared with Raj Reddy for "pioneering the design and construction of large-scale artificial intelligence systems."
