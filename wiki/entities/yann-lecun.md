## Overview

Yann LeCun is a French-American computer scientist who won the Turing Award in 2018 (with Yoshua Bengio and Geoffrey Hinton) for foundational work on deep learning. As VP and Chief AI Scientist at Meta (2013-2025), he directed Fundamental AI Research (FAIR). In late 2025, he left Meta to found [[entities/ami-labs]], raising $1.03 billion in seed funding — the largest in European history — to build [[concepts/world-models]] based on [[concepts/jepa]].

## Key Facts

- **Type**: person
- **Affiliation**: AMI Labs (founder/Executive Chairman), NYU (Silver Professor)
- **Notable for**: Convolutional neural networks, JEPA, the most vocal critic of LLMs as the path to AGI
- **Left Meta**: Late 2025 due to philosophical disagreements about AI direction and desire to pursue world model research at scale

## Key Contributions

### Intellectual Framework
- Proposed [[concepts/jepa]] in "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (2022)
- Argues LLMs are fundamentally "word models" not "world models"
- Predicts the 2020s will be the "decade of robotics" via world model + interaction learning
- Famous quote: "A system trained on language alone will never approximate human intelligence, even if trained from now until the heat death of the universe"

### Technical Work
- Pioneered convolutional neural networks (LeNet, 1989)
- Led development of I-JEPA, V-JEPA at Meta FAIR
- Introduced energy-based models for [[concepts/self-supervised-learning]]

### AMI Labs Vision
- Build systems that "understand the physical world, have persistent memory"
- Replace next-token prediction with representation-space prediction
- Physical world models enabling causal reasoning and hierarchical planning
