## Overview

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act is the world's first comprehensive legal framework for AI, establishing binding requirements for AI systems based on their risk level. It has extraterritorial reach, applying to any organization serving EU customers regardless of where the organization is based.

## Risk Classification

| Tier | Examples | Requirements |
|------|----------|--------------|
| **Unacceptable** | Social scoring, manipulative AI | Prohibited |
| **High Risk** | Medical devices, employment screening, credit decisions | Technical docs, data governance, human oversight, conformity assessment, post-market monitoring |
| **Limited Risk** | Chatbots, recommendation systems | Transparency obligations |
| **Minimal/No Risk** | Spam filters, AI in games | Unrestricted |

## Timeline

- **August 1, 2024**: Entered into force
- **February 2, 2025**: Prohibited practices and AI literacy obligations
- **August 2, 2025**: GPAI provider compliance; governance rules
- **August 2, 2026**: Full enforcement by European Commission
- **August 2, 2027**: High-risk systems in existing regulated products

## Key Requirements

For high-risk systems:
- Technical documentation maintenance
- Data governance and quality standards
- [[concepts/human-in-the-loop|Human oversight]] mechanisms
- Documented [[concepts/red-teaming]] and adversarial testing
- Conformity assessment before market deployment
- Post-market monitoring

For GPAI providers:
- Technical documentation
- Training data summary publication
- Reasonable risk mitigation policies
- Systemic risk notification to authorities

## Enforcement

Penalties up to **EUR 35 million or 7% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher. This makes non-compliance potentially more costly than GDPR violations.
