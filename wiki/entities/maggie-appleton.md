## Overview

Maggie Appleton is a designer, illustrator, and digital anthropologist who has become the primary chronicler and theorist of the [[concepts/digital-garden]] movement. Her essay "A Brief History & Ethos of the Digital Garden" is the canonical reference for the movement's origins, philosophy, and defining patterns.

## Key Contributions

### Digital Garden History and Patterns
Appleton traced the concept from Mark Bernstein's 1998 "Hypertext Gardens" through Mike Caufield's 2015 "The Garden and the Stream" to the 2018-2020 movement. She identified six core patterns: topography over timelines, continuous growth, imperfection and learning in public, playful experimentation, intercropping/content diversity, and independent ownership.

### Epistemic Status Markers
Appleton popularized the horticultural metaphor for note maturity: seedling (rough), budding (developing), and evergreen (well-developed) — providing a vocabulary for the [[concepts/learning-in-public]] aspect of digital gardens.

### Her Own Digital Garden
Appleton maintains her own digital garden at maggieappleton.com, serving as both documentation of the philosophy and a demonstration of its practice.
