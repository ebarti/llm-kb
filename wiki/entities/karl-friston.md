## Overview

Karl J. Friston FRS is a British neuroscientist and theoretical physicist at University College London (UCL). He is the originator of the [[concepts/free-energy-principle]], arguably the most ambitious attempt at a unified theory of brain function. His work has created one of the most rigorous mathematical bridges between neuroscience and artificial intelligence.

## Key Facts

- **Type**: person
- **Affiliation**: University College London, Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging
- **Notable for**: Free energy principle, predictive coding, active inference, statistical parametric mapping (SPM)
- **Impact**: Most cited neuroscientist in history (by some metrics)

## Key Contributions

1. **[[concepts/free-energy-principle]]**: A mathematical framework proposing that all adaptive systems minimize variational free energy — unifying perception, action, learning, and attention under one principle
2. **[[concepts/predictive-coding]]**: Formalized the theory of the brain as a hierarchical prediction machine that minimizes prediction errors
3. **[[concepts/active-inference]]**: Extended the FEP to explain behavior — organisms act to confirm their predictions, not just to maximize reward
4. **Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM)**: The most widely used software for analyzing brain imaging data (fMRI, PET, EEG)
5. **Dynamic Causal Modeling**: Methods for inferring effective connectivity between brain regions

## Relevance to AI

Friston's work has profound implications for AI:
- The mathematical identity between variational free energy and the ELBO (Evidence Lower Bound) used in VAEs means his brain theory and modern generative AI share the same mathematical foundation
- Active inference provides a principled alternative to standard reinforcement learning
- Predictive coding suggests architectures that process only surprises, not full inputs
- The [[concepts/bayesian-brain]] framework motivates probabilistic AI approaches
