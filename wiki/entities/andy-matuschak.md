## Overview

Andy Matuschak is a software engineer and independent researcher known for his work on tools for thought and the [[concepts/evergreen-notes]] framework. Previously at Apple (working on iOS) and Khan Academy, he now works independently on how technology can help people think and learn.

## Key Contributions

### Evergreen Notes
Matuschak's framework for notes that "evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects." Five principles: atomic, concept-oriented, densely linked, associative ontologies over hierarchies, written for self. The core insight: "Better note-taking misses the point; what matters is better thinking."

### Public Note System
His notes are publicly accessible at notes.andymatuschak.org, implemented as a sliding-pane system where clicking a link opens the linked note in an adjacent pane. This pioneered the visual paradigm later adopted by tools like Obsidian and Andy Mode in Logseq.

### Spaced Repetition Research
Matuschak has also contributed to [[concepts/spaced-repetition]] research, exploring the intersection of memory systems and knowledge building. He explicitly distinguishes spaced repetition (which aids retention) from evergreen notes (which build ideas), while noting that the regular maintenance of linked notes provides organic spaced exposure to past ideas.

### "Tools for Thought" as Research Program
Matuschak (often with collaborator Michael Nielsen) has articulated a research program around "tools for thought" — exploring how software can augment human intellectual capabilities, continuing the lineage from [[entities/vannevar-bush]]'s memex through Doug Engelbart's augmentation framework.

## Influence

His work heavily inspired the design of bidirectional linking in [[entities/obsidian]], [[entities/roam-research]], and [[entities/logseq]]. His emphasis on concept-oriented notes and associative organization directly influenced the networked note-taking movement that exploded in 2020.
