## Overview

The military AI competition between the US and China represents the highest-stakes dimension of the [[concepts/us-china-ai-race]]. Both nations are investing heavily in AI-enabled military capabilities, but they approach the challenge from fundamentally different positions.

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | United States | China (PLA) |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| **Operational Experience** | Extensive (Ukraine, Gaza, Iran, 2022-2026) | None since 1979 |
| **Key Demonstration** | Operation Epic Fury: 900 strikes in 12 hours | No combat demonstrations |
| **AI Targeting Systems** | Gospel, Lavender, "Where's Daddy", Maven | Under development |
| **LLM Integration** | GenAI.mil (classified Grok) across all DoD | Unknown |
| **Infrastructure Spend** | Project Maven ($480M+), ARIA | Not publicly disclosed |
| **Doctrine** | AI-augmented existing doctrine | "Intelligentized warfare" (AI-first) |
| **Primary Contractors** | Palantir, OpenAI, xAI | State-owned enterprises |
| **Chip Dependency** | Nvidia (domestic production) | Huawei Ascend + stockpiled Nvidia |
| **Algorithmic Efficiency** | Standard compute-heavy models | DeepSeek-style efficiency advantage |
| **Autonomy Goal** | Human-in-the-loop (official policy) | "Fully autonomous combat decisions" |
| **Vulnerability** | Private company ethical constraints (Anthropic removal) | Zero real-world testing/validation |

## Key Asymmetries

### US Advantages
1. **Operational feedback loop**: Years of classified deployments refined through live conflicts provide irreplaceable data
2. **Integrated systems**: Maven, GenAI.mil, and targeting tools work across unified command structures
3. **Demonstrated capability**: Operation Epic Fury proved AI can compress the decision cycle from weeks to hours
4. **Alliance network**: NATO integration and allied intelligence sharing

### China Advantages
1. **Algorithmic efficiency**: DeepSeek-style models require less compute, potentially enabling more distributed military AI
2. **Domestic chip independence**: Huawei integration provides baseline capability without US supply chain risk
3. **Doctrinal ambition**: "Intelligentized warfare" envisions AI as the organizing principle, not an add-on
4. **No ethical constraints**: Goal of "fully autonomous combat decisions" without democratic oversight pressure

## Critical Assessment

The US advantage is "real" but "not permanent" ([[sources/nanonets-ai-warfare-pentagon-china-2026]]). The historical analogy is Britain's Chain Home radar in 1940 — the advantage lay in the integrated system (radar + fighter control + communication), not isolated technology. Similarly, the US military AI advantage comes from integrated operational systems refined through combat, which takes years to replicate.

China's critical gap — zero combat testing — means its systems are untested against the fog of war, adversary countermeasures, and real-world edge cases. Simulation cannot fully substitute for operational experience.

## When Each Approach Matters

| Scenario | Advantage |
|----------|-----------|
| Near-term conflict | US (operational experience, proven systems) |
| Long-term capability building | China (doctrinal ambition, efficiency) |
| Autonomous operations | China (fewer ethical constraints) |
| Allied operations | US (NATO integration) |
| Compute-constrained scenarios | China (DeepSeek efficiency) |
| Full-spectrum warfare | US (integrated systems across domains) |
