## Overview

Three of the most influential thinkers in AI have published strikingly different analyses of where AI is heading. [[entities/dario-amodei]] (CEO, Anthropic) focuses on what powerful AI could achieve for humanity. [[entities/leopold-aschenbrenner]] (former OpenAI researcher, hedge fund founder) frames AGI as an imminent national security crisis. [[entities/ilya-sutskever]] (former OpenAI Chief Scientist, SSI founder) argues the scaling paradigm is exhausted and novel research is required. Their convergences and divergences define the intellectual landscape of the AGI debate in 2026.

## Analysis

### Where They Agree

1. **Transformative potential**: All three agree AI will be among the most consequential technologies in human history
2. **Safety matters**: All three prioritize alignment/safety, differing on mechanism and urgency
3. **Near-term arrival**: Even Sutskever's lower bound (5 years) puts transformative AI within a decade
4. **Current approaches are insufficient**: Amodei emphasizes risk management, Aschenbrenner security, Sutskever research paradigm

### Where They Diverge

**Mechanism of progress**: The deepest disagreement. Aschenbrenner believes continued compute scaling will produce AGI; Sutskever believes this approach is fundamentally exhausted and novel learning methods are required. Amodei implicitly sides closer to Aschenbrenner (Anthropic invests heavily in infrastructure) but hedges more.

**Speed of transformation**: Aschenbrenner's intelligence explosion (<1 year from AGI to superintelligence) is dramatically faster than Amodei's "compressed 21st century" (5-10 years). Sutskever offers no public timeline for the AGI-to-superintelligence transition.

**Framing**: Aschenbrenner's national security framing ("the free world's very survival") is qualitatively different from Amodei's positive-sum vision ("this world is worth fighting for") or Sutskever's research focus. These frames shape very different policy recommendations.

**Role of government**: Aschenbrenner predicts and seemingly endorses US government "nationalization" of AGI development. Amodei proposes a democratic coalition strategy. Sutskever is silent on government involvement.

### Who Has the Best Track Record?

**Sutskever**: The strongest technical credentials. Co-created AlexNet, architected the GPT scaling paradigm, led OpenAI's Superalignment team. His pivot away from scaling carries unique weight.

**Aschenbrenner**: The most specific, falsifiable predictions (AGI by 2027). As of April 2026, some predictions are tracking (massive infrastructure buildout, security concerns becoming mainstream) while AGI has not arrived.

**Amodei**: The most institutional credibility as CEO of a frontier lab. His timeline (late 2026-2027) is about to be testable. His company's actual model releases (Claude series) provide real-time evidence of capability progress.

## When to Apply Each Framework

- **For strategic planning**: Aschenbrenner's urgency is most useful — if AGI arrives by 2027, preparation must begin now
- **For understanding potential benefits**: Amodei's five-domain framework is the most detailed positive vision
- **For technical research direction**: Sutskever's "end of scaling" thesis is most relevant — what to build next
- **For policy analysis**: Combine Aschenbrenner's security focus with Amodei's democratic coalition strategy
- **For investment decisions**: All three imply massive value creation, but with very different risk profiles
