## Overview

The three most prominent humanoid robot programs represent distinct strategies: [[entities/tesla-optimus]] bets on manufacturing scale and FSD AI transfer, [[entities/figure-ai]] on rapid iteration and proprietary VLA, and [[entities/boston-dynamics]] on decades of robotics expertise combined with Google DeepMind AI. Each targets different market segments with different timelines.

## Analysis

### Tesla Optimus: Manufacturing Scale Play

Tesla's competitive advantage is manufacturing at scale. The company targets $20-30K pricing, which would make Optimus dramatically cheaper than competitors. The strategic decision to share AI with FSD gives access to massive driving data and neural network expertise. However, demonstrations have been heavily criticized for relying on teleoperation rather than autonomous capability, and Rodney Brooks calls the vision "pure fantasy thinking." The June 2025 leadership change (Autopilot head taking over) signals a deeper FSD integration strategy.

### Figure AI: Venture-Funded Sprint

Figure AI has moved fastest in hardware iteration (three generations in three years) and made the boldest AI pivot, abandoning OpenAI's language models for proprietary high-rate motor control ([[entities/helix-vla]]). The $39B valuation on $1.7B raised reflects extreme market optimism. The BotQ factory-of-robots-built-by-robots is ambitious. Risks include burn rate at startup scale, the safety lawsuit, and unproven manufacturing at volume.

### Boston Dynamics Atlas: Engineering Excellence

Boston Dynamics brings 30+ years of robotics engineering excellence and the most sophisticated kinematic capabilities. The DeepMind Gemini partnership adds frontier AI reasoning. However, the $140-150K price point limits the market to high-value enterprise applications. The Hyundai backing provides manufacturing capacity but the company has historically struggled with commercial deployment despite impressive demos.
