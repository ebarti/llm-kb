## Key Points

- Obsidian: 95% responsiveness, <200ms latency at 20K notes; local-first markdown; native Zettelkasten support
- Notion: 98% responsiveness online but 70% offline; cloud-based; Agents feature (Sep 2025) for autonomous actions
- Logseq: 90% responsiveness, 250ms latency; block-based outliner; AGPL open-source
- Obsidian removed commercial license requirement in early 2025 — free for all use
- Notion moved AI to Business tier ($20/mo) in May 2025; Plus plan gets only 20 AI trials
- Obsidian Bases (November 2025) adds native database views, closing gap with Notion databases
- Logseq v1.5.0 improved queries and knowledge graph visualization

## Detailed Summary

This comparison crystallizes the three dominant PKM philosophies for 2026. Obsidian's local-first, plugin-extensible model has become even more compelling: free for business use, 2,700+ plugins, 1.5M active users, and the Bases feature addressing its main weakness (no native database views).

Notion's AI pivot — locking AI behind the $20/mo Business tier — may alienate individual users who were its core growth engine. Meanwhile, Obsidian's AI story is entirely community-driven (100+ AI plugins) with no vendor tax, aligning with the [[concepts/file-over-app]] philosophy.

Logseq occupies a distinct niche: its block-level referencing is more granular than Obsidian's page-level linking, making it superior for certain Zettelkasten workflows. However, its smaller ecosystem and lower performance limit adoption.

The performance data (Obsidian at 200ms with 20K notes) validates it as a viable platform for large LLM-KB vaults — the wiki structures described in this knowledge base would perform well within those bounds.
