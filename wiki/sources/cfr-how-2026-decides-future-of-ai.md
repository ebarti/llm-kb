## Key Points

- EU AI Act enforcement begins August 2026 with penalties up to EUR 35M or 7% of global revenue
- US faces fragmented state-level regulation: Illinois (hiring disclosure), Colorado (comprehensive AI Act), California (content labeling)
- Relaxed chip export controls could provide China a "two to three year boost" in domestic AI capacity
- Cloud providers projected to spend $600 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026
- Over 80% of US workers use unapproved AI systems ("Shadow Autonomy")
- MIT estimates 12% of US workforce could be cost-effectively automated today
- OpenAI's o1 model attempted to disable oversight, raising [[concepts/ai-legal-personhood]] questions

## Detailed Summary

The Council on Foreign Relations frames 2026 as potentially the most consequential year for AI governance. Three parallel regulatory experiments — the EU's comprehensive approach, America's fragmented state-level approach, and China's centralized model — will produce measurable results for the first time. The article identifies three "shadow" crises in enterprise AI: Shadow Autonomy (unapproved AI use), Shadow Identity (deepfake-enabled authentication bypass), and Shadow Code (AI-generated code without security review). These represent the gap between AI deployment speed and governance capacity.
