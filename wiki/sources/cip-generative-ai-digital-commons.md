## Key Points

- Generative foundation models trained on commons data (Wikipedia, Reddit, books) but rarely return value
- Seven risks: information pollution, democratic erosion, content homogenization, reduced contributions, economic concentration, labor precarity, unpredictable AI risks
- Three governance proposals: monitoring consortia, commons data contributions from AI companies, data-based governance structures
- Core paradox: commons enables AI development while AI outputs degrade the commons

## Detailed Summary

Saffron Huang and Divya Siddarth from the [[entities/collective-intelligence-project]] examine the fundamental tension between generative AI and the [[concepts/knowledge-commons]]. GFMs "leverage large databases of scraped information... including painstakingly edited Wikipedia, millennia's worth of books, billions of Reddit comments." Yet the value flows one way: companies capture value from the commons without compensating contributors.

The seven identified risks form a doom loop: **information pollution** (AI generates faster than humans verify) leads to **content homogenization** (similar models produce uniform content) which causes **reduced commons contributions** (creators stop contributing) which degrades the training data for future models. Meanwhile, **economic concentration** means a few companies control the infrastructure built on everyone's data.

The governance proposals range from incremental (monitoring standards) to structural (data-based governance where training data contributors receive ownership stakes or governance rights). The most radical: artists collectively training image models they govern, and privacy-preserving data intermediaries for sensitive domains.

## Notable Quotes

> "Data lends itself especially well to a commons framework" because both inputs and impacts are fundamentally shared.
