## Key Points

- [[entities/sora]] shut down March 24, 2026 due to unsustainable unit economics: ~$15M/day compute costs, $2.1M total lifetime revenue, per-clip cost of $1.30
- The market consolidated into four tiers: quality-first ([[entities/runway]] Gen-4), cost-efficiency ([[entities/kling]] 3.0 at $0.07/sec), ecosystem ([[entities/veo]] 3 with native audio), and creative (Pika 2.5)
- [[entities/veo]] 3 introduced native synchronized audio generation -- a breakthrough that eliminates entire post-production steps
- [[entities/kling]] 3.0 introduced multi-shot sequences (2-6 scenes) with character lock consistency and native dialogue with lip sync
- AI video market projected at $847M in 2026, growing to $3.35B by 2034 at 34.2% CAGR
- VC investment reached $4.7B in 2025, a 189% increase from 2023
- Successful platforms prioritized sustainable unit economics over subsidized growth

## Detailed Summary

Digital Applied's analysis provides the most complete picture of the AI [[concepts/video-generation]] landscape following Sora's dramatic shutdown. The failure of Sora -- which burned $15M daily on compute while generating only $2.1M in total lifetime revenue -- represents a watershed moment for the industry, demonstrating that raw model capability without sustainable economics is insufficient.

The surviving market leaders each occupy distinct niches. Runway Gen-4 targets professional production with character persistence, motion capture, and in-video editing. Kling 3.0 offers 65% cost reduction with multi-shot sequences and automatic scene transitions. Veo 3's native audio generation creates synchronized sound effects, dialogue, and ambient audio from text prompts alone.

Pricing has compressed rapidly: Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.05/second makes AI video generation economically viable for high-volume production. A marketing agency producing 100 ten-second videos monthly pays approximately $50 with Veo Lite versus $130 with Sora's unsubsidized pricing.
