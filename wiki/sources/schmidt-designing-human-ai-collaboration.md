## Key Points
- "The most powerful AI-native experiences treat AI as a partner — transparent, personal, controllable, resilient, and trustworthy"
- Five principles: Transparency, Personalization, Control, Resilience, Trust
- Control should shift fluidly between human and AI based on context and preference
- "Trust — not attention — is the true currency underlying every successful product"
- Figma Make exemplifies the principles: NL prompts + full creative control + AI suggestions awaiting approval

## Detailed Summary

Christian Marc Schmidt (Schema Design) provides a concise, practitioner-oriented framework for [[concepts/human-ai-collaboration]] that synthesizes across the field. His five principles map directly to product design decisions:

**Transparency** aligns with [[concepts/explainable-ai-ux]]: mark AI content, explain reasoning, provide sources, communicate limitations. The "peek under the hood" metaphor connects to the Governors patterns from [[sources/shapeof-ai-ux-patterns]].

**Personalization** addresses [[concepts/personalization-in-ai]] with a key constraint: act like "a helpful concierge rather than feeling invasive." Obtain consent, provide feedback mechanisms, prevent filter bubbles.

**Control** is the most nuanced principle: it is not that humans must always be in control, but that control should **shift fluidly** between human and AI based on context. For routine tasks, AI takes the lead; for critical decisions, humans do. This is a more sophisticated model than the binary "human in the loop" framing.

**Resilience** addresses the inevitable reality that AI makes mistakes. Design must support iterative refinement, clear limitation communication, and recovery pathways. This connects to [[concepts/hallucination-contamination]] — resilient systems prevent errors from compounding.

**Trust** is cumulative — built through consistent experience over time, not features alone. Schmidt's assertion that "trust, not attention, is the true currency" reframes AI product strategy.
