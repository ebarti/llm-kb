## Key Points

- AI fundamentally alters how ideas, data, and decisions flow through groups and institutions
- Two historically separate approaches — design-minded practitioners (convenings) and model-minded researchers (simulations) — can be bridged by AI
- "Vibe teaming" experiments show AI scaffolding better human performance rather than replacing it
- Four key research questions about when AI enhances vs. distracts in collaborative settings

## Detailed Summary

Jacob Taylor and Scott E. Page argue that generative AI is not just a productivity tool but a force reshaping the "physics" of [[concepts/collective-intelligence]] — the flows and dynamics of group cognition. They identify a long-standing divide between two camps: **design-minded practitioners** (psychologists, anthropologists) who run collaborative workshops, and **model-minded researchers** (economists, systems scientists) who build simulations. Both address complex policy coordination but speak "distinct cultures and languages."

AI serves as a **translation engine**: it can capture and structure real-time deliberation, connect it to simulations, and create feedback loops where "rooms inform models, and models provide decision support back to collaborative groups." The authors reference "vibe teaming" — AI functioning as an integrated teammate, not a peripheral tool.

Their practical example involves green energy transition: design groups develop implementation narratives while modeling teams produce probability distributions. AI could integrate these complementary perspectives into coherent policy guidance.
