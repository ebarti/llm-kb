## Key Points

- Plain text has been Sivers' practice since 1990 across every platform and device he has used
- "You will outlive these companies. Your writing should outlive you." — a direct argument against proprietary formats
- Plain text converts easily into HTML, Markdown, LaTeX, and other formats
- Sivers has written four books and four hundred blog posts entirely in plain text
- The "tranquility" of plain text comes from its offline, non-commercial nature

## Detailed Summary

Derek Sivers makes a comprehensive case for plain text as the only reliable long-term storage format for personal writing and knowledge. His central argument is about **independence**: every few years a new company offers a special format with convenience features, but at the cost of flexibility, portability, and independence. When those companies fail, your work is trapped.

Plain text satisfies all requirements simultaneously: it works on every device ever made and every device yet to be invented; it requires no internet connection; it needs no special software; and it converts trivially into any other format. Sivers views his accumulated writings as "extended memory" and "a history of my life" — making durability a personal imperative, not just a technical preference.

His approach is radically minimalist: folders for organization, date and tag fields for metadata, and a basic text editor. No databases, no apps, no cloud services.
