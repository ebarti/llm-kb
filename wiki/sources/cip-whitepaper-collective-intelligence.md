## Key Points

- Identifies a "Transformative Technology Trilemma": capitalist acceleration, authoritarian technocracy, or shared stagnation
- Proposes collective intelligence R&D as a fourth path combining progress, participation, and safety
- Two pillars: value elicitation mechanisms and remade technology institutions
- Key projects include Collective Constitutional AI with Anthropic and Alignment Assemblies

## Detailed Summary

The [[entities/collective-intelligence-project]] (CIP), founded by Divya Siddarth and Saffron Huang, argues that technology governance faces an impossible choice among three failed paths: unregulated market-driven development (sacrifices safety), centralized technocratic control (sacrifices participation), or local-control stagnation (sacrifices progress).

Their proposed solution is **collective intelligence R&D** — building institutional mechanisms that surface and aggregate diverse values while creating new organizational structures for technology development. Value elicitation tools include [[concepts/wisdom-of-crowds]] mechanisms like quadratic voting, liquid democracy, deliberative platforms (Pol.is), citizens' assemblies, and prediction markets. Institutional innovations include CI Corporations, benefit cooperatives, DAOs, and data intermediaries.

CIP's most notable project is **Collective Constitutional AI** with [[entities/anthropic]]: a representative sample of ~1,000 Americans drafted a constitution for Claude through a deliberative process. This demonstrates that [[concepts/ai-alignment-democratic]] can be operationalized at scale.
