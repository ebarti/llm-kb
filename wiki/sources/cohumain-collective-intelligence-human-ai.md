## Key Points

- COHUMAIN: Collective Human-Machine Intelligence framework
- Transactive Systems Model with three interlocking systems: Memory (TMS), Attention (TAS), Reasoning (TRS)
- Human-only teams currently outperform human-AI teams in information sharing
- AI can undermine collective intelligence by displacing human memory, removing autonomy, creating over-reliance
- Instance-Based Learning Theory (IBLT) proposed as compatible cognitive architecture for collaborative AI

## Detailed Summary

Gupta, Nguyen, Gonzalez, and Woolley address the question: "How do we know that a sociotechnical system as a whole is exhibiting [[concepts/collective-intelligence]]?" Their COHUMAIN framework identifies four core problems across two dimensions: between-member metacognition (perceiving mental states, recognizing distributed knowledge) and between-member interactions (aligning mental states, coordinating actions).

The Transactive Systems Model (TSM-CI) comprises three systems. The **Transactive Memory System** manages distributed knowledge — AI can accelerate expertise discovery. The **Transactive Attention System** coordinates focus — but AI managing attention entirely risks "undermining collective cognition." The **Transactive Reasoning System** aligns goals with environmental changes.

A sobering finding: **human-only teams consistently outperform human-AI teams** in information sharing efficiency. This suggests current AI design fails to integrate with collective cognitive processes. The authors propose Instance-Based Learning Theory as a bridge — encoding actions as memory instances compatible with human bounded rationality.

Key warning: AI agents lack higher-order goal autonomy. Their objectives are designer-set, potentially misaligning with human collaborators' actual interests.
