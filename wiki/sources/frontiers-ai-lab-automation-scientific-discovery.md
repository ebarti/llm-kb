## Key Points

- AI transitioning from "co-pilot" (interpreting knowledge) to "lab-pilot" (conducting experiments)
- AlphaFold-2 earned a Nobel Prize in Chemistry for protein structure prediction
- Halicin: novel antibiotic scaffold discovered by deep neural networks
- ISM001-055: first AI-generated small molecule to reach Phase II clinical trials (Insilico Medicine, 2024)
- GNoME: DeepMind engine predicted stability of 380,000 unknown crystals
- A-Lab: autonomous laboratory for solid-state synthesis of inorganic powders
- SysRev: over 16,000 systematic review projects enabled by AI in 5 years
- GPT-4-driven agents now design, execute, and interpret multi-step chemical reactions independently

## Detailed Summary

Thomas Hartung's review article in Frontiers in AI traces the arc of AI's transformation of scientific research. The key framing is a progression from AI as a **co-pilot** (helping researchers interpret existing knowledge) to a **lab-pilot** (actively conducting experiments and generating new knowledge).

In **literature synthesis**, platforms like SysRev have enabled over 16,000 systematic review projects, democratizing access to cutting-edge research. In **hypothesis generation**, AlphaFold-2's Nobel Prize-winning protein structure predictions and the discovery of halicin (a novel antibiotic) demonstrate AI's ability to propose research directions that human scientists had not considered.

The most dramatic frontier is **autonomous experimentation**: GPT-4-driven agents now design, execute, and interpret chemical reactions independently. Insilico Medicine's ISM001-055, the first AI-generated drug to reach Phase II clinical trials, marks a milestone. DeepMind's GNoME predicted 380,000 unknown crystal structures, and A-Lab operates as a fully autonomous chemistry laboratory.

The article emphasizes governance concerns: alignment with the EU AI Act and ISO 42001, reproducibility of AI-driven results, equitable access to advanced technologies, and algorithmic bias mitigation.
