## Key Points

- Ontology = "a set of concepts and categories in a subject area or domain that shows their properties and the relations between them"
- Knowledge model hierarchy: Controlled Vocabulary → Thesaurus → Taxonomy → Ontology → Knowledge Graph (each level adds representational power)
- Unlike taxonomies (single-parent trees), ontologies allow multi-parent inheritance, reflecting real-world complexity
- Three relationship types: equivalence (different terms for same concept), hierarchical (parent-child), associative (cross-taxonomy connections)
- Ontologies function as "master data management for AI" -- structural frameworks encompassing workflows and business processes beyond traditional MDM
- Scalability benefit: changes made once propagate automatically through associative relationships, eliminating redundant recoding
- Case study: Cleveland Museum of Art developed ontology connecting geo-spatial data with behavioral analytics for visitor experience personalization
- More granular ontologies deliver more meaningful AI responses and enable conversational commerce through semantic deconstruction

## Detailed Summary

Earley Information Science presents ontology as the foundational knowledge structure for enterprise AI, positioning it above taxonomies in the knowledge organization hierarchy. The key insight is that ontology-based AI systems make inferences based on defined content relationships, not just ML pattern matching, enabling both personalized and explainable results.

The knowledge model hierarchy (vocabulary → thesaurus → taxonomy → ontology → knowledge graph) provides a maturity continuum for organizations. Each level adds representational power: controlled vocabularies standardize terms, thesauri add synonymy, taxonomies add hierarchy, ontologies add cross-taxonomy relationships, and [[concepts/knowledge-graph]] instances capture specific relationship data.

The Cleveland Museum of Art case study is notable as a non-tech-industry example: the museum built an ontology connecting physical spaces, visitor behavior, and collection characteristics to personalize experiences. This demonstrates ontology's applicability beyond traditional enterprise IT.
