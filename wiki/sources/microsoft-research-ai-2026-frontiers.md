## Key Points

- Peter Lee: "Every research scientist has AI lab assistants that suggest and run parts of experiments"
- EvoDiff: designing proteins never seen in nature from billions of sequences
- Project Ex Vivo: bridging computation and experimentation for cancer cell targeting
- Virtual patients: digital twins simulating disease progression and treatment responses
- Katja Hofmann: interactive storytelling where audiences co-create narratives with AI
- Yan Lu: "agentic media" — dynamic communication channels replacing static documents
- Tanuja Ganu: AI for underserved populations (rural teachers, farmers, health workers)
- Jina Suh: psychological stewardship embedded in AI design
- 20 research frontiers spanning science, healthcare, creativity, economics, robotics, and social impact

## Detailed Summary

Microsoft Research's 2026 vision document is the most comprehensive institutional roadmap for AI applications beyond code. It identifies 20 research frontiers organized around the theme of reconstructing core computing principles rather than grafting AI onto existing frameworks.

**Scientific discovery** is the flagship application: Peter Lee envisions AI lab assistants that suggest and run experiments. Ava Amini's team treats biology as a language, with EvoDiff designing entirely novel proteins and Project Ex Vivo targeting cancer cells through computational-experimental integration.

**Precision healthcare** represents the clinical frontier: Hoifung Poon aims to create "virtual patients" — digital twins that simulate disease progression by integrating radiology, pathology, and genomics. Xinxing Xu describes multimodal foundation models for clinical agentic systems with clinician-in-the-loop validation.

**Creative applications** receive serious attention: Katja Hofmann envisions interactive storytelling as a shared creative space, while Yan Lu proposes "agentic media" that transforms static documents into dynamic, context-sustaining communication channels. This directly extends [[concepts/post-code-ai-workflow]] beyond developers to all knowledge workers.

**Inclusive innovation** is a recurring theme: Tanuja Ganu focuses on serving underserved populations through multilingual, context-aware interfaces. Venkat Padmanabhan pursues synthetic multilingual data for under-resourced languages and human-expert-in-the-loop systems like AashaBot for health workers.

The document is notable for treating **psychological well-being** as a first-class research concern: Jina Suh advocates embedding psychological stewardship into AI design to mitigate dependency and social fragmentation.
