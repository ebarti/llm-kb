## Key Points

- Without reinforcement, we lose 50% of new information within an hour and 90% within a week (forgetting curve)
- Spaced repetition + active recall: adaptive scheduling where successful recalls extend intervals, failures shorten them
- Critical challenge: breaking knowledge into flashcards risks losing structural context
- Solution: structural cards (hierarchy, connection, sequence, context) that preserve how concepts interconnect
- Anki remains "the most sophisticated spaced repetition platform available"
- FSRS algorithm uses machine learning to optimize individual review schedules
- "The best spaced repetition system is the one you actually use"

## Detailed Summary

This source addresses a fundamental tension in knowledge management: the gap between understanding and retention. PKM systems like [[concepts/zettelkasten]] and [[concepts/evergreen-notes]] excel at building connections and generating insights, but they don't ensure you can recall that knowledge when needed. Spaced repetition bridges this gap.

The forgetting curve is stark: without reinforcement, most information is lost within a week. Spaced repetition combats this through adaptive scheduling — reviewing material at progressively longer intervals as mastery develops. Combined with active recall (forcing retrieval rather than passive review), this moves information from short-term to long-term memory efficiently.

The article's most valuable contribution is addressing the **structure preservation problem**. Naive flashcard creation breaks knowledge into disconnected fragments. The solution is creating structural cards alongside content cards: hierarchy cards showing how sections relate, connection cards testing relationships between concepts, sequence cards for processes, and context cards showing where information fits in broader arguments.

The tool landscape has Anki at the center, with its FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) algorithm representing a significant advance over older SM-2 algorithms by using machine learning to optimize for individual performance patterns. RemNote bridges note-taking and spaced repetition, while Obsidian plugins integrate spaced repetition directly into PKM workflows.
