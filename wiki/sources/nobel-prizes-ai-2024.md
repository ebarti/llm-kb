## Key Points

- Physics 2024: John Hopfield (Hopfield networks) and Geoffrey Hinton (Boltzmann machines, backpropagation, deep learning) — for machine learning using artificial neural networks.
- Chemistry 2024: David Baker (computational protein design, Rosetta, RFdiffusion) and Demis Hassabis + John Jumper (AlphaFold 2, protein structure prediction).
- First time AI researchers won Nobel Prizes in both Physics and Chemistry in the same year.
- Sparked debate about AI's disciplinary identity and its role in fundamental science.

## Detailed Summary

The [[concepts/nobel-prizes-ai-2024|2024 Nobel Prizes]] represent a watershed for [[concepts/ai-for-scientific-discovery]]. The Physics prize recognized the foundational work that made modern AI possible: Hopfield's associative memory networks (1982, based on spin glass physics) and [[entities/geoffrey-hinton]]'s Boltzmann machines, backpropagation popularization, and deep learning foundations.

The Chemistry prize honored two complementary achievements: [[entities/david-baker]]'s decades of computational protein design (leading to Rosetta, RFdiffusion, and the Institute for Protein Design) and [[entities/demis-hassabis]] and Jumper's [[entities/alphafold]] 2, which solved the 50-year protein structure prediction challenge.

The connection between the prizes illustrates AI's intellectual lineage: AlphaFold 2 was made possible only through Hopfield's and Hinton's foundational work on neural networks.
