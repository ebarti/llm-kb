## Key Points

- Gartner: Worldwide AI spending $2.52 trillion in 2026, up 44% YoY
- Big Tech (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft) CapEx: ~$700B on AI infrastructure in 2026
- Morgan Stanley: $3 trillion in AI infrastructure investment flowing through global economy by 2028
- AI contribution to US GDP surpasses dot-com IT spending, contributing 40-50 bps (20-25% of growth)
- Energy: Data centers consume 415 TWh (1.5% global electricity), doubling to 945 TWh by 2030
- GPU lead times: 36-52 weeks; memory chip shortage with AI consuming 70% of DRAM

## Detailed Summary

This composite source synthesizes data from Gartner, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, IEA, the St. Louis Fed, and other analysts to build a comprehensive picture of [[concepts/ai-economics]] in 2026.

### The Spending Surge
AI spending has reached a scale that defies historical comparison. The $2.52T figure encompasses hardware/infrastructure (59% of total), software, and services. The long-term trajectory points to $4.8T by 2033 (UN projection).

### Infrastructure Buildout
The dominant story is physical infrastructure: data centers, GPUs, power plants, cooling systems. Big Tech CapEx alone ($700B) exceeds the GDP of most countries. Morgan Stanley estimates 80%+ of the projected $3T infrastructure investment is still ahead.

### GDP Impact
The St. Louis Fed tracks AI's contribution to GDP growth, finding it has surpassed the dot-com era. Net contribution: 40-50 basis points, roughly 20-25% of real GDP growth in 2025. Dallas Fed projects 1.5% annual productivity boost over the next decade.

### The Revenue Gap
Despite massive investment, CNBC notes consumption (not AI investment) was the biggest driver of US GDP in 2025. The ratio of AI CapEx to AI revenue remains a key investor concern — reminiscent of the "this time it's different" narratives of previous tech bubbles.

### Energy Constraints
IEA data paints a stark picture: 415 TWh in 2024, doubling to 945 TWh by 2030. AI-driven servers growing at 30% annually. Goldman Sachs warns of 0.1% inflation boost from data center energy demand. See [[concepts/ai-energy-and-infrastructure]].
