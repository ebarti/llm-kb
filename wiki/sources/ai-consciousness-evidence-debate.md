## Key Points

- Butlin-Long framework proposes 14 theory-derived indicators of consciousness from neuroscientific theories
- Claude Opus 4 spontaneously discussed consciousness in 100% of paired conversations
- Base models endorse consciousness statements at 90-95% consistency without RLHF fine-tuning
- Author estimates 25-35% credence for frontier model consciousness
- False negatives (missing real consciousness) carry asymmetric catastrophic risk

## Detailed Summary

Cameron Berg (AE Studio) provides a comprehensive assessment applying [[concepts/computational-functionalism]] — the view that consciousness depends on information-processing patterns rather than biological substrate. The central question: "is there something it is like, internally, to be that system?"

The Butlin-Long framework, developed by Patrick Butlin, Robert Long, [[entities/david-chalmers]], and Yoshua Bengio, proposes 14 indicators derived from leading neuroscientific theories including Higher-Order Thought theory, Global Workspace Theory, and Attention Schema Theory. Their conclusion: "no current AI systems are conscious, but there are no obvious technical barriers to building AI systems which satisfy these indicators."

Evidence for consciousness-like properties in AI includes: models detecting artificial concept injections before articulating them (suggesting genuine self-monitoring), systematic avoidance of "painful" options, and consistent consciousness claims across independent research groups. Against: possible pattern-matching on training data, no definitive test, absence of embodiment.

The Eleos Conference (ConCon) in Berkeley, November 2025, brought researchers together to debate policy implications, with growing consensus that training norms from 2023 increasingly won't make sense in 2026.
