## Key Points

- US chips approximately 5x more powerful than China's best; gap widens to 17x by 2027
- Huawei Ascend 910C achieves only 60% of H100 real-world performance
- Huawei's 2026 next-gen chip will be *less* powerful than current models (SMIC constrained to 7nm)
- Huawei produces only 4-5% of Nvidia's computing output even in aggressive scenarios
- Nvidia: 4.5M chips in 2025, scaling to 10M+ by 2027 (TSMC advantage)
- Exporting 3M H200 chips to China would provide computing power China couldn't produce domestically until 2028-2029
- Core argument: "Huawei is not a threat that justifies loosening controls; it is evidence that the controls are working"

## Detailed Summary

This CFR analysis makes a data-driven case for maintaining [[concepts/ai-chip-export-controls]]. The performance gap between [[entities/nvidia]] and [[entities/huawei]] is not just wide — it is widening due to SMIC's manufacturing constraints at 7nm versus TSMC's advanced nodes. Even Huawei's roadmap shows regression rather than progress, suggesting fundamental manufacturing bottlenecks that capital alone cannot solve.

The production capacity asymmetry is even more striking: Nvidia's exponential scaling versus China's linear growth means the gap compounds over time. The policy recommendation is unambiguous: the H200 export decision risks providing China with years of compute capacity it could not produce domestically.
