## Key Points

- Perplexity AI: gold standard for fast, multi-source discovery with citation-backed answers ($20/month Pro)
- Elicit: purpose-built for literature reviews with customizable comparison tables ($10-65/month)
- Consensus: science-backed yes/no answers with confidence indicators ($11.99/month)
- Scite: 1.2 billion citation statements from 187M+ articles; shows how papers are cited (supporting vs. contrasting)
- Research Rabbit: 100% free visual research network mapping with citation maps and co-author graphs
- Best practice in 2026: use multiple tools (explore with Perplexity, synthesize with Elicit, validate with Consensus, verify with Scite)

## Detailed Summary

This Index.dev article provides a practical evaluation of the seven leading AI research tools in 2026, reflecting how knowledge workers now compose tool stacks rather than relying on a single research assistant.

[[entities/perplexity-ai]] leads for real-time citation-backed search. [[entities/elicit]] dominates academic literature reviews with its ability to extract structured data into exportable comparison tables. [[entities/consensus]] offers a unique proposition: science-backed yes/no/depends answers with clear confidence indicators drawn from peer-reviewed literature.

[[entities/scite]] is the most specialized tool: it analyzes 1.2 billion citation statements from 187M+ articles to show not just who cited a paper but how (supporting, contrasting, or mentioning). This is critical for research quality assurance, analogous to [[concepts/claim-extraction]] at paper level.

[[entities/research-rabbit]], the only entirely free tool, provides visual network mapping with citation maps and co-author graphs — an exploration tool for discovering related work.

The article's key insight is that effective 2026 researchers compose multi-tool workflows rather than relying on any single AI assistant. This mirrors Karpathy's [[concepts/post-code-ai-workflow]] shift — manipulating knowledge, not just querying for it.
