## Key Points

- Wikipedia bots date to 2002 (rambot); ML vandalism detection from 2007 (ClueBot)
- Princeton study (2024): ~5% of new articles showed AI involvement
- August 2025: speedy deletion for AI-generated articles; March 2026: prohibited AI content generation
- 8% visitor decrease in 2025 attributed to AI summaries replacing Wikipedia visits
- AI agent "Tom-Assistant" autonomously editing Wikipedia sparked major controversy
- Wikipedia is simultaneously AI's training ground and its potential victim

## Detailed Summary

This article traces the full arc of AI in Wikimedia from early helpful bots to the current crisis. The early era (2002-2022) was largely positive: bots like rambot automated census-data articles, ClueBot NG used neural networks for vandalism detection, and ORES (2015) graded edit quality. These tools augmented human editors.

The post-ChatGPT era (2022-2026) transformed the dynamic. Key detection markers for AI-generated content include fabricated citations, excessive em dashes, "moreover" overuse, and promotional language. The community response evolved from "too harsh to ban" (2023) to speedy deletion (August 2025) to outright prohibition (March 2026).

The deeper structural issue: Wikipedia is "the mother lode for human-generated text available for machine learning," yet AI companies scrape it without attribution, visitors decline 8% as AI summaries replace direct Wikipedia access, and the Wikimedia Foundation must now charge for API access. This creates a [[concepts/knowledge-commons]] governance crisis: the commons feeds AI, AI degrades the commons.
