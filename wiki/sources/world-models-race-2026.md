## Key Points

- The world models paradigm exploded into mainstream AI in late 2025/early 2026, with over $1.3B in combined funding
- [[entities/ami-labs]] raised $1.03B at $3.5B valuation — the largest European seed round in history
- [[entities/genie]] 3 achieved real-time 24fps interactive world generation at 720p
- [[entities/nvidia-cosmos]] hit 2M downloads within a year of launch, trained on 20M hours of footage
- [[entities/world-labs]] (Fei-Fei Li) launched Marble with VR support and game engine export
- World model inference requires 8-32 GPUs/request (vs 1-8 for LLMs), indicating much higher compute demands
- [[entities/yann-lecun]] argues LLMs are fundamentally insufficient for AGI; world models are the necessary path

## Detailed Summary

The article maps the competitive landscape of world model development in early 2026, identifying four major players. AMI Labs, founded by Turing Award winner Yann LeCun after leaving Meta in late 2025, represents the most philosophically ambitious effort — aiming to replace LLMs entirely with systems that learn physical world representations via [[concepts/jepa]]. DeepMind's Genie 3 takes a more practical approach, building real-time interactive 3D environments for agent training. NVIDIA's Cosmos focuses on industrial applications for [[concepts/physical-ai]] (autonomous vehicles, robotics), while Fei-Fei Li's World Labs targets creative applications with Marble.

The competitive video generation landscape also reflects world model thinking: Runway's Gen-4.5 (#1 on Video Arena) explicitly frames itself around world modeling, while OpenAI's Sora 2 fell to #7 despite pioneering the "video as world simulation" framing.

Infrastructure requirements are substantial: world model training demands thousands to tens of thousands of GPUs, petabyte-scale storage, and high-bandwidth memory (H200, B200 GPUs) for real-time inference.
