## Key Points
- EU AI Act: binding law, 4 risk tiers, penalties up to EUR 35M or 7% global revenue
- NIST AI RMF: voluntary, 4 functions (Govern/Map/Measure/Manage), sector-agnostic
- ISO/IEC 42001: certifiable international standard, lifecycle-focused
- EU AI Act full enforcement begins August 2, 2026
- High-risk systems require: technical docs, data governance, human oversight, conformity assessment
- Organizations can create unified compliance programs covering all three frameworks

## Detailed Summary

This comparison lays out the three frameworks that define AI governance in 2025–2026. The EU AI Act is the most consequential, with extraterritorial reach and severe penalties. It classifies AI systems into four risk tiers: unacceptable (prohibited), high (heavily regulated), limited (transparency required), and minimal (unrestricted).

NIST's AI RMF provides a voluntary but widely referenced framework organized around four functions: Govern (organizational environment), Map (context understanding), Measure (risk analysis), and Manage (risk response). ISO/IEC 42001 adds a certifiable management system standard.

For organizations operating across jurisdictions, the integration strategy is practical: inventory and classify AI systems, map controls across frameworks, establish governance, and implement iteratively — prioritizing high-risk EU systems.
