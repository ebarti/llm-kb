## Key Points

- Idea generation and brainstorming experienced the largest increase in corporate AI adoption (+12%) between 2024-2025
- Quality ideation balances originality (deviation from existing ideas) and appropriateness (feasibility)
- LLMs support creativity through two pathways: persistence (exhaustive variations) and flexibility (distant concept combination)
- Individual creativity improves, but group-level diversity paradoxically narrows when everyone uses similar tools
- Four proposed AI ideation roles: Designer, Writer, Interviewer, Actor

## Detailed Summary

Harvard Business Review argues that generative AI's greatest promise extends beyond productivity to unlocking "new forms of human creativity that can drive innovation and growth." The key insight is that intentional deployment matters: how you use the LLM determines whether you get productivity gains or genuine creative breakthroughs.

The article proposes four distinct roles for AI in ideation. As a **Lead Ideator**, the AI can serve as "The Designer" (creating personalized variants and multivariate testing) or "The Writer" (elevating perceived quality and persuasiveness). As a **Thought Partner**, it becomes "The Interviewer" (asking probing questions that reveal blind spots) or "The Actor" (simulating customer responses, with caveats about representation).

Practical techniques for enhanced creativity include persona modifiers ("think like Steve Jobs"), hybrid prompting with varied parallel prompts, [[concepts/chain-of-thought-prompting]] for step-by-step generation, and temperature adjustments. For domain-specific creativity, [[concepts/fine-tuning]] on specialized content and [[concepts/rag-prompting]] with domain data are recommended.
