## Key Points

- 12 people worldwide with severe paralysis have received Neuralink implants as of September 2025
- Neuralink plans high-volume production and automated surgery in 2026
- Fundamental tension: medical device company vs. transhumanist venture
- The Link: 23mm diameter, 8mm thick, reads electrical signals from neurons
- China approved its first invasive BCI (Neuracle Technology) in March 2026

## Detailed Summary

The article examines [[entities/neuralink]]'s evolution from research project to near-commercial BCI platform. As of late 2025, twelve patients with ALS and quadriplegia use brain-computer interfaces to communicate, type, and control digital tools through thought alone.

The core tension lies between two visions. The medical path focuses on restoring function to severely disabled patients — a legitimate, regulated medical device. The transhumanist path, championed by [[entities/elon-musk]], envisions "machine-human symbiosis" and consumer-grade cognitive enhancement. Competitors argue this dual messaging hampers the entire BCI industry's ability to secure regulatory approval and insurance coverage.

Regulatory concerns include Neuralink's recruitment of a senior FDA official who had been overseeing the company's regulation. Meanwhile, China entered the BCI market with Neuracle Technology receiving marketing approval for a spinal cord injury implant.
