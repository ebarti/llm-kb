## Key Points

- AI-assisted stories evaluated as more creative, better written, and more enjoyable
- Less creative writers benefit disproportionately from AI assistance
- AI-enabled stories are more similar to each other than human-only stories
- Fundamental tension: individual improvement vs. collective homogenization
- Implications extend beyond creative writing to research, analysis, and ideation

## Detailed Summary

This Science Advances paper provides the most rigorous empirical evidence for what might be called the **AI creativity paradox**: generative AI makes each individual creator better while making the collective output less diverse.

The experimental design compared stories written with and without access to AI-generated ideas. AI-assisted stories were consistently rated higher on creativity, writing quality, and enjoyment. The effect was strongest for **less creative writers**, for whom AI acted as an equalizer, raising the floor of creative output.

However, the critical finding is that AI-enabled stories were **more similar to each other** than stories produced by humans alone. When everyone draws from the same well of AI-generated patterns, the collective output converges. Writers are individually better off, but the cultural ecosystem becomes less varied.

This paradox has implications far beyond creative writing. The same dynamic applies to any domain where LLMs are used for knowledge manipulation: [[concepts/llm-legal-applications]] (legal briefs may converge), [[concepts/ai-research-assistants]] (research directions may narrow), [[concepts/llm-education-tutoring]] (educational content may homogenize). Organizations using AI for brainstorming may generate better individual ideas but less diverse portfolios.

The paper suggests that **deliberate strategies** are needed to maintain intellectual diversity in an AI-assisted world — varying prompts, using different models, incorporating diverse human perspectives alongside AI output.
