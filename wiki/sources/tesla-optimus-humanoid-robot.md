## Key Points

- Announced August 2021; Gen 2 (Dec 2023, 11 DOF hands); Gen 3 (Oct 2024, 22 DOF hands)
- Specs: 5'8", 125 lb, 45 lb carrying capacity
- Uses same AI system as Tesla Full Self-Driving (FSD)
- Production plan: few hundred in 2026 (factories + partners), thousands from 2027-2028
- Long-term price target under $20K; early models $25-40K
- Program head Milan Kovac resigned June 2025; replaced by Autopilot head Ashok Elluswamy
- Heavily criticized: demos relied on teleoperation, Rodney Brooks calls it "pure fantasy thinking"

## Detailed Summary

[[entities/tesla-optimus]] is Tesla's general-purpose [[concepts/humanoid-robots]] project, announced at AI Day 2021. Elon Musk claimed it would handle "anything humans don't want to do" and could be "more significant than the vehicle business."

The hardware evolved through three generations. The initial 2022 prototypes were rudimentary (one walked across a stage). Gen 2 (December 2023) brought a slimmer design with 11-DOF hands, demonstrated dancing and cooking. Gen 3 (October 2024) upgraded to 22-DOF hands with 3 DOF in wrist/forearm, using planetary roller screw actuators for walking stability.

The critical technical decision was sharing the AI system with Tesla's [[concepts/autonomous-driving]] Full Self-Driving platform, connecting the robot's perception and planning directly to the Autopilot neural networks. The June 2025 leadership change -- replacing Kovac with Autopilot head Elluswamy -- reinforced this FSD-first approach.

Production is planned to begin summer 2026 with a few hundred units for Tesla factories and select enterprise partners. Musk has mentioned 50,000 units for 2026, though this conflicts with other estimates of "a few hundred." Consumer availability remains years away.

The project has faced sustained criticism. Multiple demonstrations were exposed as relying heavily on teleoperation rather than autonomous control. Roboticist Rodney Brooks dismissed the humanoid robot vision as "pure fantasy thinking" in 2025. Bloomberg called it "mission creep," and The Washington Post noted Tesla's history of "exaggerating timelines." Despite criticism, the sheer scale of Tesla's manufacturing capability and FSD data pipeline make Optimus a serious long-term contender.
