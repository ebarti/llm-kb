## Key Points

- Gartner: Worldwide AI spending forecast at $2.5 trillion in 2026, a 44% increase over 2025
- Jensen Huang: $3-4 trillion on AI infrastructure by end of decade
- Goldman Sachs consensus: $527 billion Big Tech 2026 capex
- Amazon: $200B (up from $131B in 2025) — the capex leader
- Google: $175-185B (up from $91B in 2025)
- Meta: $115-135B; $600B US infrastructure commitment through 2028
- Microsoft: 2 GW nuclear commitment with Constellation Energy through 2040
- Stargate Initiative: $500B joint venture — largest single AI infrastructure project
- OpenAI-Cerebras: $10B+ contract for 750 MW of compute through 2028
- Foundational AI startups raised $178B in Q1 2026 alone (vs $88.9B all of 2025)
- Inference at two-thirds of total AI compute spending by 2026
- Federal Reserve published analysis of AI's global trade effects (February 2026)

## Detailed Summary

The scale of AI infrastructure investment in 2025-2026 is historically unprecedented. Gartner's $2.5 trillion worldwide AI spending figure for 2026 includes not just hardware but also software, services, and operations. The more revealing figure is Big Tech capex: Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft alone are projected to spend over $527 billion on capital expenditures in 2026, with the overwhelming majority directed at AI infrastructure.

The investment has become large enough to reshape macroeconomics. The Federal Reserve published a formal analysis of AI's impact on global trade flows. Power utilities are scrambling to add generation capacity. Real estate markets near data center hubs are transformed. The question is no longer whether this investment is real but whether the returns will justify it — the classic infrastructure bubble-or-foundation debate.
