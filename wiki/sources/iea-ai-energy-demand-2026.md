## Key Points

- 2024 baseline: ~415 TWh data center electricity (~1.5% of global)
- 2026 projection: 1,100 TWh — equivalent to Japan's entire national electricity consumption
- 2030 base case: ~945 TWh (~3% of global), growing 4x faster than overall electricity demand
- AI-accelerated servers: 30% annual growth vs 9% for conventional servers
- Accelerated servers account for ~50% of net consumption increase
- Inference has surpassed training as the dominant energy consumer at fleet scale
- US: +240 TWh by 2030 (130% increase); projected 1,200 kWh per capita for data centers alone
- Virginia grid operators issued capacity warnings through 2028; Northern Virginia halted new permits
- TDP per chip: 700W (H100) to 1,000W+ (B200/B300)
- Liquid cooling adoption expected at 47% of server racks by 2026

## Detailed Summary

The International Energy Agency's analysis paints a picture of AI as a transformative force on global energy infrastructure. The headline figure — 1,100 TWh by 2026, equivalent to Japan — captures the scale, but the structural details matter more. AI-accelerated servers are growing at 30% annually while conventional servers grow at 9%, meaning AI's share of data center energy is rapidly increasing even as the overall pie expands.

The shift from training to inference as the dominant energy consumer has profound implications. Training is concentrated in a few large clusters; inference is distributed everywhere, making energy efficiency at inference time a higher-leverage problem. The industry response includes nuclear power commitments (Microsoft's 2 GW deal), solar farms (Amazon's 1.5 GW), and liquid cooling (Google's 30% power reduction in TPU v6 clusters).
