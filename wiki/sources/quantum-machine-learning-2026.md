## Detailed Summary

Quantum machine learning sits at a fascinating inflection point in 2026. The theoretical promise is extraordinary: quantum computers could explore exponentially large feature spaces, solve optimization problems intractable for classical machines, and simulate quantum systems relevant to drug discovery and materials science. The practical reality is more modest: current NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) devices have hundreds of qubits where enterprise AI needs thousands or millions.

The pragmatic response is hybrid architectures: quantum processors handle the computations where they offer an advantage (feature mapping, kernel evaluation, optimization) while classical computers manage everything else. This approach mitigates hardware limitations while establishing the software and algorithmic foundations for future full-quantum systems.
