## Key Points

- AI-based enterprise search uses LLMs and semantic understanding to interpret user intent, replacing keyword matching with context-aware retrieval
- An Enterprise Graph is the foundational architecture: a dynamic knowledge model linking people, data, and processes that continuously learns from user interactions
- Leading platforms integrate with 100+ SaaS applications with real-time indexing
- Security is non-negotiable: real-time permission checks, audit logs, role-based access controls, GDPR/SOC 2 compliance
- Code intelligence is a specialized application: knowledge graphs cross-reference code with documentation and organizational knowledge
- AI agents now execute multi-step workflows (onboarding, compliance checking, debugging) beyond simple search
- The market is $6.83B (2025), projected $11.15B by 2030 at 10.30% CAGR

## Detailed Summary

This guide from [[entities/glean]] presents enterprise search as a foundational infrastructure layer, not just a product feature. The key innovation is the Enterprise Graph -- essentially a [[concepts/knowledge-graph]] tailored to organizational structure. Unlike general knowledge graphs, an Enterprise Graph encodes relationships between people, their roles, the documents they create and access, and the processes they participate in.

The competitive landscape includes [[entities/glean]] (Enterprise Graph + broad integration), Moveworks (agentic AI for IT service management), Coveo (personalized recommendations for digital experience), Elastic (open-source customizable search), and Guru (governed knowledge management with trust scoring).

The guide emphasizes that enterprise search is evolving beyond search into agentic workflow automation -- AI agents that don't just find information but take action across platforms. This aligns with the broader trend identified in [[sources/ek-km-trends-2026]] of conversational AI replacing traditional search.
