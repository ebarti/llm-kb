## Key Points

- Europe has ~30% more AI talent per capita than the US, ~3x more than China
- Net tech talent inflows to Europe collapsed from 52,000 (2022) to 26,000 (2024)
- US salaries 30-70% higher: $140-210K (US) vs $90-150K (W/N Europe) vs <$100K (S/E Europe)
- 57% of European AI professionals completed undergrad outside Europe
- Young, highly cited scholars 100x more likely to move to industry than 10-year veterans
- Global AI talent demand exceeds supply 3.2:1
- 72% of employers report hiring difficulty (ManpowerGroup 2026)
- Government initiatives: US $2.8B (AI Research Institutes), EU EUR 1.6B (Digital Europe), China targeting 500K new AI professionals by 2027

## Detailed Summary

This Euronews investigation documents the paradox at the heart of Europe's AI strategy: the continent trains disproportionate AI talent but cannot retain it. The salary gap with the US is the primary driver (30-70% differential), but equity compensation, research infrastructure, and startup ecosystem vibrancy also play roles.

The data reveals both intra-European brain drain (smaller countries losing to Switzerland, Netherlands, UK) and transcontinental flows (primarily to the US, with Gulf countries as an emerging destination). The academic brain drain is particularly acute, with young high-citation researchers overwhelmingly preferring industry roles.
