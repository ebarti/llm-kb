## Key Points

- China's 2017 State Council AI Development Plan targets 1 trillion yuan AI economy by 2030
- Over 2,100 government guidance funds with $1.86 trillion in target size
- Beijing pledged 1 trillion yuan ($137 billion) over five years for AI supply chain
- Computing vouchers worth ~$300,000 per company across 17 provinces
- DeepSeek transitioned from hedge fund to AI lab after Beijing's 2021 investment crackdown
- DeepSeek secured 10,000 A100 GPUs before export restrictions took effect
- Zhejiang University (DeepSeek founder's alma mater) received $3 billion in R&D grants
- Liang Wenfeng: "money has never been the problem... bans on shipments of advanced chips are the problem"

## Detailed Summary

Lawfare's analysis explains that [[entities/deepseek]]'s success is not an isolated event but the product of a comprehensive [[concepts/ai-industrial-policy]] ecosystem. China has created a multi-layered support structure: national-level strategic plans, provincial competition for AI talent and prestige, public-private investment vehicles, tax incentives, computing subsidies, and government contracts that create virtuous cycles of funding and data access.

The article identifies Hangzhou's "City Brain" project as a paradigmatic example of B2G partnerships creating feedback loops. However, it also acknowledges systemic vulnerabilities: misallocated capital, market distortions, and the fundamental constraint of US [[concepts/ai-chip-export-controls]].
