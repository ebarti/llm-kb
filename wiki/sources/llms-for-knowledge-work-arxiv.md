## Key Points

- LLM work adoption: 24.5% (Summer 2023) → 34.6% (Fall 2024)
- Four use categories: Creation, Information Tasks (most common), Advice, Automation (emerging)
- 60% of work-related LLM use occurs within larger workflows; only 11% use LLMs for multiple steps
- Information tasks most common: search, learning at appropriate complexity levels, summarization, analysis
- 70% of workers want automation capabilities (calendar, expense reports, scheduling)
- Strong interest in querying organizational knowledge bases with LLMs
- Trust issues: "I fundamentally don't trust the work of people who I know use LLMs"
- Key finding: LLMs reshape knowledge work incrementally, not transformatively (so far)

## Detailed Summary

This longitudinal study tracked how knowledge workers adopted and used LLMs across two survey waves (Summer 2023 and Fall 2024). The most striking finding is the gap between current use and desired use. Workers currently use LLMs primarily for **information tasks** (search, learning, summarization) and **creation** (drafting code, emails, content) — but what they want most is **automation** and **data-integrated knowledge management**.

The desire for organizational knowledge bases accessible via LLM is particularly relevant to PKM research. Workers described wanting to "train a bot with all guild content to answer FAQs" — essentially describing the [[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]] pattern. The gap between this aspiration and current tools underscores the [[concepts/knowledge-base-product-gap]].

Trust emerged as a significant barrier. One worker stated: "I fundamentally don't trust the work of people who I know use LLMs...because I know how likely it is the work is poor quality." This trust deficit, combined with hallucination concerns and lack of repeatability, suggests that the [[concepts/hallucination-contamination]] problem is not just theoretical — it actively limits adoption.

The paper's key conclusion — that LLMs are reshaping knowledge work incrementally rather than transformatively — contrasts with the more ambitious visions of [[concepts/agentic-knowledge-management]] and suggests the gap between what's possible and what's adopted remains wide.
