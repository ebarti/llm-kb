## Key Points

- Photonic chips use photons instead of electrons — data at speed of light with no resistive heat loss
- LightGen (Science, 2026): all-optical chip achieving 100x speed and energy efficiency over electronic chips for vision generation
- MIT photonic processor: DNN computations in under half a nanosecond, 92%+ accuracy
- Neurophos: first commercial photonic processor running ResNet, BERT, and reinforcement learning
- Three manufacturing platforms: indium phosphide (InP), silicon nitride (SiN), silicon photonics (SiPh)
- Complete revolution by 2026 unrealistic; ~300 million units projected by 2030, 1 billion by 2040
- Near-term adoption focus: data center optical interconnects replacing electrical links between GPU clusters
- Co-packaged optics (CPO) was the top discussion topic at OFC 2026
- China investing heavily in photonic AI chips as route around US export controls (Nature, 2026)

## Detailed Summary

[[concepts/photonic-computing]] represents perhaps the most fundamental paradigm shift in computing since the transistor — replacing electrons with photons as the computational medium. The physics are compelling: optical signals carry no charge, generate no resistive heat, and travel at light speed. For AI workloads dominated by matrix multiplications, photonic processors can perform these operations through wave interference rather than sequential arithmetic.

However, the gap between laboratory demonstrations and commercial deployment remains wide. Manufacturing complexity, integration challenges with electronic components, and lack of standardization limit near-term adoption. The most practical 2026 application is optical interconnects — replacing electrical cables between GPU racks with photonic links that offer higher bandwidth at lower power.
