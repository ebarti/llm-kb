## Key Points

- Evergreen notes are "written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects"
- Five principles: atomic structure, concept-oriented, densely linked, associative ontologies over hierarchies, written for self
- The goal is "better thinking," not "better note-taking"
- Heavily inspired by Luhmann's Zettelkasten but uses distinct terminology to explore variations
- Evergreen notes differ from spaced repetition: the former builds ideas, the latter aids retention
- Proposes "number of evergreen notes written per day" as a leading indicator of intellectual development

## Detailed Summary

Andy Matuschak's evergreen notes concept represents a philosophy of knowledge work where notes are not disposable records of meetings or readings, but durable intellectual assets that compound over time. Unlike transient notes (which most people create), evergreen notes are designed to be revisited, revised, and connected across projects and years.

The five principles define the system. **Atomicity** ensures each note is small enough to complete in under 30 minutes, representing a single self-contained idea. **Concept-orientation** means notes are organized by the concept they discuss, not by the book or project that spawned them — this enables connections across domains. **Dense linking** creates rich associative networks that promote serendipitous discovery. **Associative ontologies** favor connection-based organization over rigid hierarchies. And **writing for self** prioritizes personal clarity over audience considerations.

Matuschak explicitly distinguishes his practice from Luhmann's [[concepts/zettelkasten]], noting that while heavily inspired by it, he uses the distinct term "evergreen notes" to explore variations without inheriting the cultural baggage of Zettelkasten discourse. He also distinguishes evergreen notes from [[concepts/spaced-repetition]] — the two serve different purposes (building ideas vs. retaining facts) and existing spaced repetition tools actively discourage the kind of incremental synthesis that evergreen notes require.

The system's key insight is that externalizing serious thinking in durable form makes creative work compound rather than dissipate. Each note is a checkpoint of solid intellectual ground, and the constant revision cycle provides tighter feedback than traditional writing practices.
