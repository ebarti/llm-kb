## Key Points

- Three primary use cases: document summarization/classification, document drafting, legal research
- AI adoption among legal professionals surged from 19% (2023) to 79% (2024) per Clio study
- Key tools: Everlaw (document analysis), Luminance (document review), Casetext (legal research), Westlaw Edge (AI-enhanced research), Gavel Exec (contract review)
- LLMs as supplementary tools enabling focus on analysis, strategic advice, and client work
- Ethical concerns: systemic bias, hallucination, unsupervised reliance risks

## Detailed Summary

Gavel's guide provides a practitioner perspective on LLM adoption in small law firms, demonstrating that the knowledge-manipulation shift Karpathy described for developers is happening in parallel across the legal profession.

**Document summarization and classification** automates traditionally paralegal-heavy tasks. Tools like [[entities/everlaw]] and [[entities/luminance]] rapidly summarize large document volumes and automatically categorize them — a direct analog to [[concepts/wiki-compilation]] in the legal domain.

**Document drafting** uses AI assistants like Gavel Exec to create initial drafts of pleadings and contracts. Attorneys shift from initial creation to refinement, mirroring the developer-to-curator transformation described in [[concepts/post-code-ai-workflow]].

**Legal research** platforms like [[entities/casetext]] and Westlaw Edge access extensive databases to identify relevant cases, statutes, and opinions. This is essentially [[concepts/llm-qa-over-documents]] applied to legal corpora.

The adoption data is striking: the Clio study shows AI adoption among legal professionals surging from 19% to 79% in a single year (2023-2024), one of the fastest adoption curves in any professional domain.
