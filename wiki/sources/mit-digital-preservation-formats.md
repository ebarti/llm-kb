## Key Points

- Five criteria for archival formats: open standards, community adoption, published documentation, lossless compression, unencrypted storage
- **Plain text (US-ASCII, UTF-8, UTF-16) is the recommended format for text content**
- Open, non-proprietary formats are more likely to remain usable long-term
- Lost passwords/keys render encrypted files permanently inaccessible
- Library of Congress maintains the Sustainability of Digital Formats reference

## Detailed Summary

MIT Libraries provides institutional validation for the plain-text-as-preservation-format argument. Their recommendation is unambiguous: for text content, the preferred format is plain text encoded in standard character sets (US-ASCII, UTF-8, UTF-16).

The five selection criteria they identify — open standards, community adoption, published documentation, lossless compression, and no encryption — are precisely the properties that markdown satisfies. Markdown IS plain text (UTF-8) with lightweight formatting conventions that require no special parser to read. It has massive community adoption, well-documented syntax (CommonMark), and no encryption or compression.

This institutional perspective complements the individual arguments from [[entities/derek-sivers]] and [[entities/steph-ango]]: whether you're a solo writer preserving personal journals or an institution preserving research outputs, plain text is the recommended choice.
