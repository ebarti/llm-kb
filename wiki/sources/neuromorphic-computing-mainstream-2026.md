## Key Points

- Intel Loihi 3: 8 million neurons, 64 billion synapses, 4nm, 1.2W peak — first commercial Intel neuromorphic chip
- IBM NorthPole: full-scale production, 72.7x more energy-efficient than GPUs for LLM inference
- Both achieve ~1,000x GPU power efficiency for robotics and sensory processing
- ANYmal D Neuro robot: 72 hours on one charge (9x improvement over GPU)
- Mercedes-Benz, BMW integrating neuromorphic vision for autonomous braking
- Global AI energy projected at 134 TWh annually by 2026 (equivalent to Sweden)

## Detailed Summary

The year 2026 marks the commercial arrival of [[concepts/neuromorphic-computing]]. [[entities/intel-loihi]] 3, built on 4nm process with 8 million digital neurons and 64 billion synapses, operates at just 1.2 watts peak load. It uses leaky integrate-and-fire dynamics with spike-timing-dependent plasticity — directly mimicking biological neurons. Intel plans commercial deployment by Q3 2026 in healthcare, autonomous vehicles, and industrial automation.

[[entities/ibm-northpole]] enters full-scale production, eliminating the von Neumann bottleneck through co-located memory and compute across 256 cores. It achieves 72.7x higher energy efficiency for LLM inference and 25x better efficiency for image recognition compared to high-end GPUs.

The energy context is crucial: global AI energy consumption is projected at 134 TWh annually by 2026 (equivalent to Sweden's total energy usage). NVIDIA GPUs draw 10-60W per chip, severely limiting untethered robotics. Neuromorphic chips offer a fundamentally different energy profile.

Historical context: Loihi 1 (2018) had 128 neuromorphic cores and 130K neurons on 14nm, already achieving 5,000x better energy-delay product. Loihi 2 offered 10x speedup on Intel 4 process. Loihi 3 represents the transition from lab to market.
