## Detailed Summary

This narrative account by Ian Fisher frames [[entities/cyc-project]] as "history's forgotten AI project" — the most thorough attempt to hand-encode human common-sense knowledge, now largely overshadowed by neural approaches.

[[entities/doug-lenat]]'s earlier work demonstrated both brilliance and ambition: AM (Automated Mathematician) rediscovered mathematical theorems comparable to Ramanujan's work, and EURISKO could modify its own heuristics and won a board game tournament through unorthodox strategy.

The 1983 Stanford meeting that catalyzed Cyc calculated that genuine machine intelligence required several million rules — approximately 2,000 person-years of manual encoding. When MCC formed in 1984 to counter Japan's fifth-generation project, Lenat secured access to a half-billion dollar budget.

"Ontological engineers" manually analyzed English text, extracting the tacit knowledge humans take for granted — pronoun resolution, implied relationships, physical causality. The system grew to 1.5 million concepts and 25 million rules feeding over 1,000 specialized inference engines, organized into microtheories.

After MCC disbanded (1994), Cycorp survived through government and corporate contracts, but the field shifted decisively toward deep learning around 2012. Machine learning proved more successful at the tasks that had motivated AI — image recognition, game-playing, language understanding — without requiring manual knowledge encoding.

The article's most poignant detail: Lenat's final paper, written before his August 2023 death, proposed integrating Cyc's auditable reasoning with LLMs' fluency. This vision — that structured knowledge and neural language models are complementary rather than competing — aligns with the broader [[concepts/neural-symbolic-integration]] trend and is precisely the insight motivating modern [[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]] systems.
