## Key Points

- Every expert group has shortened AGI timelines dramatically: 2060-2070 in 2020 to 2028-2033 by 2026
- AI company leaders (Altman, Amodei, Musk) cluster around 2026-2027
- Metaculus median forecast dropped from 50 years to ~5 years between 2020-2026
- Deep definitional disagreement persists — "AGI" means different things to different forecasters
- Yann LeCun remains the most prominent skeptic, arguing current architectures cannot achieve general intelligence
- Expert opinion "can neither rule out nor rule in AGI soon"

## Detailed Summary

This composite source draws from 80,000 Hours' review of expert forecasts, Nevo's timeline compilation, and Metaculus prediction data to paint the most complete picture of where the field stands on AGI timing as of early 2026.

### The Compression Trend

The single most striking finding: essentially every category of forecaster — academic researchers, prediction market participants, industry executives — has pulled timelines forward. This compression accelerated after ChatGPT (Nov 2022) and GPT-4 (March 2023).

### Industry Leaders vs. Researchers

AI company leaders cluster around 2026-2028, but have obvious incentive bias. Academic researchers are more conservative (median ~2032-2040) but their forecasts have been repeatedly falsified — the Python coding prediction was off by 2+ years.

### Definitional Chaos

The most significant finding may be the lack of consensus on what "AGI" means:
- [[entities/dario-amodei]]: Nobel Prize-level intellectual capability
- OpenAI: Five-level internal model (Chatbots → Organizations)
- DeepMind: Five capability tiers x autonomy levels
- Yann LeCun: Rejects the term entirely

This makes comparing predictions nearly impossible and raises the question of whether "AGI" is a useful concept at all, or whether we should focus on specific capabilities.
