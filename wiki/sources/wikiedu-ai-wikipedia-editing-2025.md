## Key Points

- 178 of 3,078 articles (5.8%) flagged as AI-generated since 2022
- Two-thirds of AI-flagged articles failed verification — plausible-sounding claims cited to real sources but information not actually present
- Only 7% contained entirely fabricated sources (subtler than outright hallucination)
- 87% of surveyed editors found AI helpful for research tasks (identifying gaps, finding sources)
- English Wikipedia adopted guidelines against AI-generated content

## Detailed Summary

LiAnna Davis at Wiki Education conducted a systematic audit of AI-generated content in their Wikipedia editing programs. Using the Pangram detection tool, they found a steady increase in AI-generated articles after ChatGPT's November 2022 launch. The critical finding: most AI-generated content fails verification not through obviously fabricated citations but through **subtle misattribution** — real sources cited for claims they do not actually contain.

This connects directly to [[concepts/hallucination-contamination]] but reveals a more insidious variant: not fabricated facts, but **plausible misattributions** that require reading the actual source to detect. This is far harder to catch at scale than outright fabrication.

However, the picture is nuanced. A survey of 102 participants showed AI tools genuinely help editors identify article gaps, locate reliable sources, and evaluate drafts. The line Wiki Education draws: AI for **research assistance** (yes) vs. AI for **content generation** (no).
