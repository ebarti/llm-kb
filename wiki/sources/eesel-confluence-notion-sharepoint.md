## Key Points

- Three platforms dominate enterprise knowledge management, each with distinct architectural philosophies: structured wiki (Confluence), flexible blocks (Notion), enterprise content management (SharePoint)
- AI capabilities are now a primary differentiator: Notion leads with autonomous agents in base plans, Confluence includes Rovo AI at Premium tier, SharePoint requires expensive Copilot add-on
- Confluence's strength is Jira integration -- 76% of customers ship projects faster when connecting Jira to Confluence
- Notion has over 100 million users and ranks #1 for knowledge bases on G2; expanding into a full work OS (Agents, Search, Calendar, Mail)
- SharePoint serves 190+ million users across 200,000 organizations with granular permissions and HIPAA compliance
- Pricing ranges from $5/user (SharePoint/Confluence basic) to $10/user (Notion Plus), with AI add-ons potentially doubling costs

## Detailed Summary

This comparison from eesel AI reveals how enterprise knowledge platforms are converging on AI while maintaining distinct identities. [[entities/confluence]] remains the structured wiki for technical teams, deeply integrated with the Atlassian ecosystem. [[entities/notion]] has evolved from a note-taking app into a comprehensive work OS, aggressively integrating AI agents. [[entities/sharepoint]] leverages its Microsoft 365 lock-in and compliance credentials for regulated enterprises.

The AI capability gap is striking: Notion includes autonomous agents in its base plan, while SharePoint charges $30/user/month extra for Copilot. This pricing dynamic may drive enterprises to multi-platform strategies -- SharePoint for compliance-heavy areas, Notion or Confluence for collaborative knowledge work.
