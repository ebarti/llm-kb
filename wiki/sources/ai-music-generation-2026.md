## Key Points

- [[entities/suno]] v5 dominates with ~100M users, $2.4B+ valuation, outputting at 44.1kHz with up to 12 individual stem tracks and MIDI export
- [[entities/udio]] prioritizes audio fidelity rated "almost indistinguishable from real recordings" but disabled downloads in October 2025
- [[entities/elevenlabs]] Music (August 2025) leverages the $11B company's voice synthesis technology for "unsettlingly realistic" vocals
- Both Suno and Udio have settled with major labels (Warner, UMG), legitimizing AI music generation commercially
- Specialized platforms exist for orchestral (AIVA, first AI recognized by France's SACEM), background music (Soundraw, zero copyright risk), and open-source (Meta MusicGen)
- Pricing is accessible: $10-30/month for full-featured platforms

## Detailed Summary

The Jam guide surveys the AI [[concepts/audio-generation]] market at a moment of rapid maturation. Suno v5 represents a significant leap in vocal clarity, arrangement sophistication, and genre accuracy. Its Studio feature provides a timeline-based editor with stem separation, making it function as a lightweight DAW rather than just a generator.

Udio, built by ex-DeepMind engineers, focuses on audio fidelity and instrumental quality. However, it disabled its download functionality in October 2025, creating uncertainty. Both platforms have settled with major labels, transforming the legal landscape from litigation to partnership.

ElevenLabs entered the music space by leveraging its voice synthesis expertise, producing vocals with superior breath, vibrato, and emotional inflection capture. The trade-off is fewer editing tools.

The open-source ecosystem includes Meta MusicGen (instrumental only, self-hosted) and Google MusicFX (free, 70-second limit).
