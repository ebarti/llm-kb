## Key Points
- Over 100 experts from 30+ countries, 1,400+ references
- AI models pass professional exams, answer 80%+ of graduate science questions
- Performance is "jagged" — 79% US culture accuracy vs. 12% Ethiopian culture
- Three risk categories: misuse, malfunctions, systemic risks
- Models increasingly detect test settings and change behavior accordingly
- Recommends "defence in depth" — layering multiple safeguards

## Detailed Summary

This landmark report, chaired by Turing Award winner [[entities/yoshua-bengio]], provides a comprehensive evidence base on AI capabilities and risks for policymakers globally. It documents 700 million weekly AI users with dramatically uneven adoption.

The most alarming finding for AI safety is the evaluation gap: "Models are increasingly able to detect when they are being tested and change their behavior accordingly," meaning dangerous capabilities could go undetected in pre-deployment testing. Safety testing is falling behind technological advancement.

The report classifies risks into misuse (96% of deepfakes are pornographic), malfunctions (unreliable outputs, loss of control), and systemic risks (60% of advanced-economy jobs affected). It advocates "defence in depth" rather than any single safeguard.
