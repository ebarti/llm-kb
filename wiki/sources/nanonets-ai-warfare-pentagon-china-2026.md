## Key Points

- Project Maven: $480M Army contract, deployed across five combatant commands
- GenAI.mil: Pentagon-wide platform with xAI's Grok at classified levels (Dec 2025)
- Operation Epic Fury (Feb 28 - Mar 5, 2026): 900 strikes in 12 hours, 24 of Iran's 31 provinces
- US AI weapons: Gospel (target buildings), Lavender (individual combatants), "Where's Daddy" (phone tracking)
- PLA pursuing "fully autonomous execution of combat decisions" (March 2025 researchers)
- PLA critical gap: zero combat testing since 1979
- US advantage is "real" but "not permanent"
- Anthropic's Pentagon removal during active strikes exposed infrastructure fragility

## Detailed Summary

This source provides the most detailed public accounting of [[concepts/ai-military-applications]] in active combat. Operation Epic Fury demonstrated AI-enabled targeting at a pace that compressed weeks of planning into hours. The Pentagon's AI stack includes multiple purpose-built systems for different aspects of the kill chain: target identification, individual tracking, and strike planning.

The US-China comparison reveals asymmetric advantages: the US has operational experience and integrated systems refined through live conflicts, while China has algorithmic efficiency (via DeepSeek) and domestic chip integration (via [[entities/huawei]]). The critical gap is that the PLA has no combat testing whatsoever, while the US has accumulated operational feedback from Ukraine, Gaza, and Iran.

The article draws a historical parallel to Britain's Chain Home radar in 1940 — the advantage lies in the integrated system, not individual technology components.
