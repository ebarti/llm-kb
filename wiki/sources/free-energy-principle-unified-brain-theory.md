## Key Points

- The FEP proposes all adaptive systems minimize variational free energy — an upper bound on surprise
- Connects to predictive coding: brain exchanges bottom-up prediction errors and top-down predictions
- Active inference extends to action: systems change the world to match predictions
- Negative free energy equals the ELBO used in variational autoencoders — direct bridge to AI
- Precision weighting (inverse variance) explains attention
- The FEP itself is unfalsifiable (mathematical principle); only process theories can be tested

## Detailed Summary

[[entities/karl-friston]]'s [[concepts/free-energy-principle]] is arguably the most ambitious attempt at a unified theory of brain function. The principle states that biological systems minimize variational free energy — decomposed into expected energy (prediction errors), entropy (uncertainty reduction), and KL divergence (approximation accuracy).

The framework connects directly to [[concepts/predictive-coding]], where the brain continuously exchanges ascending prediction errors and descending predictions. This architecture aligns with known cortical anatomy. [[concepts/active-inference]] extends this to behavior: rather than passively perceiving, organisms actively change their environment to match predictions, minimizing free energy through perceptual and active optimization.

The mathematical connection to AI is profound: negative free energy equals the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), the standard objective for training variational autoencoders. This creates a formal bridge between Friston's brain theory and modern generative AI.
