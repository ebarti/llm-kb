## Key Points

- 51 peer-reviewed papers (2014+) on automated Wikipedia content generation reviewed
- Nine content types generated but no system produces complete articles
- Five technical approaches: template/rule-based, topic modeling, graph-based, seq2seq, transformer-based
- Only 4 of 51 studies engaged actual Wikipedia editors
- Raises epistemic justice concerns about minority-language communities

## Detailed Summary

Reeves and Simperl's systematic review reveals a gap between AI research and Wikipedia's actual needs. Researchers have built systems generating article sections, summaries, headings, infoboxes, links, and more — but no system produces comprehensive articles. The technical trajectory moves from rule-based systems (strong control, poor scalability) through topic modeling and graph-based methods to increasingly dominant transformer approaches.

The evaluation gap is striking: **44 of 51 studies** used only computational metrics (BLEU, ROUGE). Only 4 engaged Wikipedia editors directly. Seven deployed content and monitored edits — arguably the most meaningful validation, yet "there is no guarantee that deployed content will receive edits." This disconnect between AI research methodology and collaborative knowledge community needs mirrors broader tensions in [[concepts/human-ai-collaboration]].

The most important concern: automated generation risks "replacing creative work, particularly affecting minority-language communities" where small editor pools make AI-generated content more attractive but also more damaging to contributor agency. This connects to [[concepts/knowledge-commons]] governance: who benefits from automation and who loses voice?
