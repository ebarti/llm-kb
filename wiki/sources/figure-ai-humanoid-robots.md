## Key Points

- Founded 2022 by Brett Adcock; funding trajectory: $70M seed → $675M (Bezos, Microsoft, Nvidia, OpenAI) → $1B+ Series C at $39B valuation
- Three robot generations: Figure 01 (basic bipedal) → Figure 02 (35 DOF, 16 DOF hands, dual GPUs) → Figure 03 (tactile sensors at 3g resolution, doubled camera frame rate)
- Pivoted from OpenAI partnership to proprietary [[entities/helix-vla]] system: "large language models had become a smaller problem compared to high rate robot control"
- Helix 02 (Jan 2026): full-body autonomy, trained via motion capture + simulation
- BotQ manufacturing facility targeting 12,000 humanoids/year using its own robots
- Demonstrated capabilities: loading washing machines, sorting packages, folding laundry, dishwasher loading

## Detailed Summary

[[entities/figure-ai]] represents one of the most aggressively funded [[concepts/humanoid-robots]] ventures. Brett Adcock founded the company in 2022, raising $70M in seed funding before securing $675M in February 2024 from a consortium including Jeff Bezos, Microsoft, Nvidia, Intel, and OpenAI, reaching a $2.6B valuation. By September 2025, Series C exceeded $1B with a $39B valuation.

The hardware evolved rapidly across three generations. Figure 01 was a basic bipedal robot for logistics. Figure 02 (August 2024) introduced 35 degrees of freedom, five-fingered hands with 16 DOF each, 25 kg carrying capacity, six RGB cameras with an onboard VLA model, and dual Nvidia GPUs -- triple the computing power of its predecessor. Figure 03 (October 2025) added tactile sensors detecting forces as small as 3 grams, doubled camera frame rate, 60% wider field of view, and wireless inductive charging.

The partnership with OpenAI, formed in February 2024, ended after approximately one year. Adcock explained the strategic shift: large language models were a solved-enough problem, while "high rate robot control" -- the millisecond-level motor commands needed for [[concepts/dexterous-manipulation]] -- was the real bottleneck. This led to the proprietary [[entities/helix-vla]] system, which by January 2026 achieved full-body autonomy using motion-capture data and simulation-based machine learning.

The BotQ manufacturing facility aims to produce 12,000 humanoids per year, notably using Figure's own humanoid robots in the production process.

A safety controversy emerged in November 2025 when the former head of product safety sued, alleging termination for raising concerns about robots being "strong enough to fracture a human skull."
