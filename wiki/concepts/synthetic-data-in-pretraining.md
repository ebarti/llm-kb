## Overview

As high-quality natural web data becomes scarce relative to the appetite of frontier models, synthetic data generated by existing LLMs has emerged as a key augmentation strategy. Research shows it can meaningfully accelerate pretraining convergence when used correctly — but the details of mixture ratios, generation methods, and generator model selection critically determine outcomes.

This concept specifically covers synthetic data in the **pretraining** phase. For synthetic data in fine-tuning and instruction tuning, see [[concepts/synthetic-data-generation]].

## The Optimal Mixture

[[sources/synthetic-data-llm-pretraining-study]] (the largest empirical study: 1,000+ model variants, 100K+ GPU hours) establishes:

- **30% high-quality rephrased synthetic + 70% natural web text** is optimal across scales
- This mixture achieves 5-10x speedup to target validation loss at large data budgets
- Pure synthetic data never outperforms pure natural web data
- The 30/70 ratio is remarkably consistent across model sizes (up to 3B tested) and data scales (up to 200B tokens)

## Generation Approaches

### Rephrasing (Preferred)

Taking existing web documents and having an LLM rephrase them in a high-quality or QA format. This preserves factual content while improving presentation quality. Shows no model collapse at foreseeable scales.

[[sources/nemotron-cc-nvidia]] uses differentiated rephrasing strategies:
- **Low-quality documents**: Wikipedia-style rephrasing to reduce noise
- **High-quality documents**: Four specialized approaches — Q&A pairs, distillation, knowledge extraction, organized knowledge lists

### Textbook-Style Generation (Risky)

Generating entirely new educational content mimicking textbooks. Pioneered by [[entities/microsoft-phi]]'s "Textbooks Are All You Need" approach. [[sources/synthetic-data-llm-pretraining-study]] finds this shows **model collapse patterns** at small data budgets — performance degradation consistent with theoretical predictions. Requires lower mixture ratios (33% max) and larger scales to show benefit.

## Generator Model Size

A counterintuitive finding: **8B-parameter generators consistently outperform both 3B and 70B generators**. Larger models may produce more polished but less diverse outputs, reducing the value of the synthetic data. This suggests a diversity-quality tradeoff where moderate-capability generators hit the sweet spot.

## The Learned Flywheel

[[sources/nemotron-cc-nvidia]] introduces the concept of a "learned flywheel" for synthetic data in pretraining: better models produce better synthetic data, which trains better models, which produce better synthetic data. This contrasts with static pipelines and points toward iterative, self-improving data curation systems.

Nemotron-CC generated 1.8T synthetic tokens using Mistral NeMo 12B with FP8 inference, demonstrating that synthetic generation is practical at trillion-token scale.

## Model Collapse Risks

[[concepts/model-collapse]] — the theoretical concern that models trained on their own outputs degrade over generations — appears to be format-dependent:

- **Rephrased data**: no degradation observed at tested scales
- **Textbook-style data**: shows collapse patterns, especially with limited data and high synthetic ratios

The 33% HQ rephrased + 67% CC mixture shows the **lowest projected irreducible loss**, suggesting that model collapse is not inevitable with careful mixture design.
