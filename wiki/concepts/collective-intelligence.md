## Overview

Collective intelligence (CI) is the capacity of groups to achieve outcomes that exceed the abilities of any individual member. It emerges when diverse agents — human, AI, or hybrid — contribute independent judgments, skills, or knowledge that are aggregated through structured mechanisms into group-level cognition. CI is not mere averaging: it requires institutional design, information architecture, and governance to function.

The concept spans from [[concepts/wisdom-of-crowds]] (statistical aggregation of independent estimates) through [[concepts/collaborative-knowledge-building]] (structured group knowledge creation) to sophisticated human-AI sociotechnical systems studied under the COHUMAIN framework.

## Key Ideas

### Three Pillars of Collective Cognition

The COHUMAIN research ([[sources/cohumain-collective-intelligence-human-ai]]) identifies three **transactive systems** that constitute collective intelligence:

1. **Transactive Memory** — the group's distributed knowledge: who knows what, where expertise resides, how to access it. AI can accelerate expertise discovery but risks displacing human memory formation.
2. **Transactive Attention** — coordinated allocation of the group's limited focus. AI managing attention entirely risks "undermining collective cognition."
3. **Transactive Reasoning** — aligning individual goals with collective objectives and environmental changes. AI can detect goal misalignment and prompt corrective reasoning.

### AI as Translation Engine

Taylor and Page ([[sources/brookings-ai-collective-intelligence]]) propose AI as a bridge between two historically separate approaches to collective problem-solving:
- **Design-minded** practitioners who run collaborative workshops (rich context, limited scale)
- **Model-minded** researchers who build simulations (system-wide dynamics, poor grounding)

AI creates feedback loops: deliberation data flows into models, model outputs inform subsequent collaborative sessions. This "room+model" architecture could unlock collective intelligence at unprecedented scales.

### The Trilemma of Technology Governance

The [[entities/collective-intelligence-project]] ([[sources/cip-whitepaper-collective-intelligence]]) frames CI as the escape route from an impossible choice: unregulated acceleration (sacrifices safety), centralized control (sacrifices participation), or stagnation (sacrifices progress). CI mechanisms — quadratic voting, liquid democracy, citizens' assemblies — offer a fourth path combining all three.

### Wikipedia as Paradigmatic CI

[[concepts/wikipedia-knowledge-model]] represents the largest and most successful collective intelligence system for knowledge creation. Its editorial model satisfies all conditions for [[concepts/wisdom-of-crowds]]: diversity of editors, largely independent contributions, decentralized authority, and structured aggregation through editorial processes. Yet AI now simultaneously threatens and augments this system (see [[concepts/ai-generated-content-risks]]).

## The CI Paradox in the AI Era

A fundamental tension: AI systems are trained on the outputs of collective intelligence (Wikipedia, Stack Overflow, Reddit, books) but their deployment may undermine the conditions that sustain CI:

- **Independence**: AI-generated content creates correlated errors across the information ecosystem
- **Diversity**: Content homogenization as AI produces increasingly uniform information
- **Contribution incentive**: People may stop contributing to commons when AI can generate "good enough" content
- **Verification**: AI generates faster than communities can verify, overwhelming editorial capacity

This "commons parasitism" dynamic is analyzed in [[sources/cip-generative-ai-digital-commons]].

## Collective Intelligence for AI Alignment

A promising inversion: using CI mechanisms to align AI systems with collective values. Anthropic and CIP's **Collective Constitutional AI** project had ~1,000 Americans draft a constitution for Claude through deliberative processes. This demonstrates that [[concepts/ai-alignment-democratic]] is operationally feasible — crowd-sourced values can directly shape AI behavior.
