## Overview

Learning in public is the practice of sharing your learning journey as it unfolds, rather than waiting to present polished, finished work. It involves publishing incomplete notes, half-formed ideas, and active questions — making the process of understanding visible rather than only the results.

This practice is central to the [[concepts/digital-garden]] philosophy and represents a deliberate rejection of performative expertise. By using **epistemic status markers** (seedling, budding, evergreen — or numerical confidence ratings like those on Gwern.net), practitioners signal transparently how developed each idea is, inviting collaboration and correction rather than passive consumption.

## Why It Matters for PKM

Learning in public dramatically reduces the friction between learning and sharing. In traditional blogging, the gap between "I learned something" and "I published something" is large — requiring editing, polishing, and a sense of completion. In digital gardens, that gap shrinks to nearly zero. This means:

- More knowledge gets externalized (reducing reliance on fallible memory)
- Ideas get tested against public feedback earlier
- The learning process itself becomes a reusable artifact
- Community knowledge accumulates through interconnected gardens

## Connection to AI-Maintained Knowledge

The LLM-maintained knowledge base ([[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]]) can be seen as an automated form of learning in public — the system continuously publishes its evolving understanding, with wikilinks providing the dense interconnection that digital gardens prize. The key difference is that in human digital gardens, the imperfection is a feature (showing the learning process); in AI-maintained systems, imperfection is the [[concepts/hallucination-contamination]] risk.
