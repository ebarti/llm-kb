## Overview

Transactive memory systems (TMS) describe how groups distribute knowledge across members: rather than everyone knowing everything, each person specializes while maintaining a "directory" of who knows what. The group's total knowledge exceeds any individual's because members can access each other's expertise as needed. This concept, originally from psychology (Daniel Wegner, 1987), has become central to understanding [[concepts/collective-intelligence]] and [[concepts/human-ai-collaboration]].

## Key Ideas

### The Three Transactive Systems (COHUMAIN)

The COHUMAIN framework ([[sources/cohumain-collective-intelligence-human-ai]]) extends TMS into three interlocking systems:

**1. Transactive Memory System (TMS)**
Who knows what. Members develop specialized knowledge while maintaining awareness of others' expertise. When someone needs information outside their specialty, they know who to ask.

AI impact: Can accelerate expertise discovery and facilitate skill-sharing connections. Risk: displaces human memory formation — when AI handles retrieval, people stop building their own expertise directories.

**2. Transactive Attention System (TAS)**
How the group coordinates its collective focus. Members negotiate what to attend to, when to switch tasks, and how to allocate cognitive resources across problems.

AI impact: Can help synchronize attention and reduce task-switching costs. Risk: "undermining collective cognition and performance" when AI manages attention entirely — removing human agency over focus.

**3. Transactive Reasoning System (TRS)**
How the group aligns individual goals with collective objectives and adapts reasoning to environmental changes. Members negotiate shared mental models and decision frameworks.

AI impact: Can detect goal misalignment and prompt corrective reasoning. Risk: AI agents' goals are designer-set, potentially misaligning with the group's actual interests.

### Instance-Based Learning Theory (IBLT)

The COHUMAIN researchers propose IBLT as a compatible cognitive architecture for AI agents participating in transactive systems. IBLT encodes actions as state-action-utility memory instances, supporting bounded rationality consistent with human decision-making. This enables "machine theory of mind" — AI that can model human mental states and predict behavior.

IBLT-based models predict human behavior more accurately than Bayesian approaches, suggesting cognitive compatibility may be key to effective [[concepts/human-ai-collaboration]].

### Implications for Knowledge Systems

Transactive memory theory explains why both [[concepts/wikipedia-knowledge-model]] and [[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]] work: they externalize the "directory" function. Wikipedia's categories, talk pages, and WikiProjects serve as the TMS directory for its editor community. LLM knowledge bases use index files and summaries as the directory for the LLM agent.

The risk AI poses to transactive memory also explains why simple AI insertion into collaborative workflows often reduces performance: AI disrupts the existing transactive system without building a new one.
