## Overview

Reasoning tokens (also called "thinking tokens") are the tokens generated by [[concepts/reasoning-models]] during their internal deliberation phase, before producing a final answer. They serve as the model's "working memory" or "scratchpad" for multi-step reasoning. The cost and quality of [[concepts/test-time-compute]] is directly tied to the generation and utility of these tokens.

## Visibility and Transparency

| Model | Reasoning Tokens | Visible to User? |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| OpenAI o1/o3 | Hidden chain-of-thought | No (summarized only) |
| DeepSeek R1 | Explicit reasoning chain | Yes |
| Claude 3.7/4 (extended thinking) | Thinking block | Yes |
| Claude Opus 4.6 | Adaptive (auto-engaged) | Yes |

The visibility question has [[concepts/ai-safety]] implications: hidden reasoning tokens make alignment verification harder, while visible ones enable audit and trust.

## Token Budget Control

The "thinking budget" controls how many reasoning tokens a model can generate:

- **Claude 3.7**: Configurable `budget_tokens` parameter (min 1,024).
- **Claude Opus 4.6**: Adaptive -- model autonomously decides whether to engage extended reasoning.
- **OpenAI o3**: Low/medium/high reasoning effort levels.
- **NVIDIA NIM**: Thinking-Token Limiter for latency/cost control.

### Budget-Aware Training
BudgetThinker (2025) uses special control tokens and a two-stage pipeline for precise budget control. SelfBudgeter autonomously predicts required budgets per query.

## Quality vs. Quantity

A critical finding from [[sources/chen-deep-thinking-tokens|Chen et al. (2026)]]:

**Raw token count is an unreliable proxy for reasoning quality.**

Not all reasoning tokens contribute equally. "Deep-thinking tokens" -- where the model's internal predictions undergo significant revisions across deeper transformer layers -- correlate robustly with accuracy. Regular tokens that maintain textual coherence without deep processing add length but not reasoning value.

This challenges the "more tokens = better reasoning" assumption and suggests:
- Overthinking is real: longer responses can signal verbose but shallow processing.
- Quality metrics (deep-thinking ratio) outperform length-based metrics.
- The Think@n strategy uses deep-thinking ratio for early rejection of unpromising generations.

## The Overthinking Problem

[[sources/iacobacci-thinking-budget-not-enough|Iacobacci et al. (2025)]]:
- Increasing thinking budget shows diminishing returns and plateau effects.
- Weaker models fail to benefit from extended reasoning.
- Parallel strategies (multiple short chains + aggregation) often beat one long chain.

This has practical implications for [[concepts/adaptive-compute-allocation]]: it may be better to generate many short reasoning chains and vote than to generate one very long chain.

## Scaling Properties

[[sources/anthropic-extended-thinking|Anthropic]] reports that math accuracy scales **logarithmically** with thinking tokens -- each doubling of tokens yields a fixed increment of improvement. This implies:
- Early tokens are most valuable.
- There is an economic ceiling beyond which more reasoning tokens are not cost-effective.
- Adaptive allocation ([[concepts/adaptive-compute-allocation]]) is essential for efficient deployment.
