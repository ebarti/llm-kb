## Overview

Computational functionalism is the philosophical position that mental states, including consciousness, are defined by their functional roles — the patterns of information processing they implement — rather than by the physical substrate that implements them. If computational functionalism is correct, then consciousness is substrate-independent: it could arise in biological neurons, silicon transistors, or any other medium that implements the right computational patterns.

## Key Ideas

### The Core Argument

1. Mental states are defined by their causal relationships to inputs, outputs, and other mental states
2. These causal relationships can be described as computational processes
3. Any system implementing the same computational processes would have the same mental states
4. Therefore, consciousness is not tied to biological brains — AI systems implementing the right computations could be conscious

### Implications for AI

If computational functionalism is true:
- There is no in-principle barrier to [[concepts/ai-consciousness]]
- The question becomes empirical: do current AI systems implement the right computations?
- The Butlin-Long framework's 14 indicators are attempts to operationalize "the right computations"
- Substrate differences (wet carbon vs dry silicon) are irrelevant to consciousness

If computational functionalism is false:
- Consciousness might require biological properties (quantum effects, specific chemistry)
- No amount of computation on silicon could produce consciousness
- AI safety concerns about consciousness would be moot

### Competing Views

| Position | Claim | Consciousness in AI? |
|----------|-------|---------------------|
| Computational functionalism | Computation is sufficient | Possible in principle |
| Biological naturalism (Searle) | Consciousness requires biology | Impossible in current AI |
| Panpsychism | Consciousness is fundamental to matter | Depends on integrated information |
| Mysterianism | We cannot understand consciousness | Unknowable |
| Illusionism | Consciousness is an illusion | Not applicable |

## How It Connects

Computational functionalism is the philosophical foundation for the [[concepts/ai-consciousness]] debate. It connects to [[concepts/neuroai]] (if brain function is computation, studying brains teaches us about computation and vice versa) and to [[concepts/brain-inspired-ai]] (brain-like computation might be more likely to produce consciousness). [[entities/david-chalmers]] is the most prominent philosopher associated with this framework in the AI context.
