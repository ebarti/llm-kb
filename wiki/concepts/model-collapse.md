## Overview

Model collapse is a phenomenon where language models progressively degrade when trained on data generated by other language models (or themselves), creating a feedback loop where each generation amplifies errors and narrows the output distribution. As the web corpus becomes saturated with AI-generated content, this risk becomes increasingly relevant to all LLM training pipelines.

Model collapse is distinct from [[concepts/catastrophic-forgetting]] (which occurs during fine-tuning on new tasks) and [[concepts/hallucination-contamination]] (which occurs in knowledge base maintenance). All three share the theme of knowledge degradation but operate through different mechanisms.

## How It Happens

1. **Generation 0**: Model trained on human-generated web data
2. **Generation 1**: AI-generated content enters the web; new models train on mixed human + AI data
3. **Generation 2+**: Progressively more AI-generated training data; models learn to imitate model outputs rather than reality
4. **Collapse**: Models converge on a narrow, homogenized distribution, losing the long tail of human expression and knowledge

Per [[sources/ai-training-2026-synthetic-human-data]]: "If every new AI system is trained and re-trained on the same finite corpus, and then we start generating synthetic data from those same models without care, we drift toward model collapse — models learning to imitate their own and each other's mistakes."

## The Exhausted Web Corpus

The web corpus that trained major foundation models (GPT-3, GPT-4, LLaMA, DeepSeek) is effectively exhausted — no new large-scale source of human-generated text remains. This means:
- Future model improvements must come from better data curation, not more data
- [[concepts/synthetic-data-generation]] is necessary but carries collapse risk
- Domain-specific human data (decisions, conversations, expert judgments) becomes the critical competitive asset

## Mitigations

### 1. Human-Data Anchoring
Maintain a "Golden Corpus" of human-verified, human-generated examples as an immovable anchor. All synthetic data is evaluated against this standard.

### 2. Human-in-the-Loop Filtering
The flywheel approach: models generate → humans rapidly filter (accept/reject/edit) → human decisions become training signal. This scales human judgment while preventing unbounded model-on-model drift.

### 3. Lineage Tracking
Track whether each training example is human-generated, synthetic, or filtered-synthetic. This enables detecting when synthetic data ratios become dangerous and rolling back to human-anchored subsets.

### 4. Real-World Validation
Validate on real-world production tasks, not benchmarks. Benchmark performance can be maintained even as models lose practical capability on messy, real-world inputs.

### 5. Diversity Enforcement
Explicitly measure and enforce diversity in synthetic datasets — topic diversity, style diversity, reasoning pattern diversity. Homogenization is an early warning signal.

## Connection to LLM Knowledge Bases

In a [[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]] system, model collapse creates a specific risk chain:
1. LLM compiles wiki from raw sources (potentially introducing errors)
2. Wiki content becomes training data for fine-tuning
3. Fine-tuned model generates future wiki content
4. Each cycle amplifies initial errors

The mitigation in KB context: maintain raw sources as immutable ground truth, audit LLM-compiled content against originals, and never fine-tune solely on LLM-generated wiki content without human verification.
