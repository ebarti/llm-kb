## Overview

Synthetic data generation uses large language models to programmatically create training datasets, bypassing the cost and bottleneck of human annotation. This technique has become one of the most important developments in modern ML, enabling the creation of small models that rival much larger ones (see [[entities/microsoft-phi]]) and providing the data needed for [[concepts/fine-tuning]] on domain-specific tasks.

The approach exists on a spectrum: at one end, simple prompt-based generation creates instruction-response pairs; at the other, sophisticated pipelines combine generation, evolution, filtering, and human validation into flywheel loops that continuously improve data quality.

## Core Methods

### 1. Distillation-Based Generation
A stronger model (e.g., GPT-4) generates training data that a weaker model learns from. This is the most common approach and underpins [[concepts/knowledge-distillation]]. The key constraint: output quality is bounded by the teacher model's capabilities — unless combined with rigorous filtering, as demonstrated by [[entities/microsoft-phi]]'s phi-4, which surpassed its teacher on STEM benchmarks.

### 2. Self-Improvement Generation
Models iteratively generate and refine their own training data. This approach risks amplifying biases and errors, potentially leading to [[concepts/model-collapse]] if not carefully controlled with human anchoring.

### 3. Human-Synthetic Flywheels
The 2026 best practice per [[sources/ai-training-2026-synthetic-human-data]]: models generate candidates → humans rapidly filter (accept/reject/edit) → human decisions train next generation. This scales human judgment rather than replacing it.

## The Five-Step Pipeline

Per [[sources/synthetic-data-generation-llms]], a production synthetic data pipeline follows:

1. **Document Chunking** — Split source documents into segments matching production retrieval settings
2. **Context Generation** — Group semantically related chunks via cosine similarity
3. **Query Generation** — Generate questions answerable from contexts (reverse retrieval)
4. **Query Evolution** — Iteratively increase complexity (in-depth), diversity (in-breadth), and quality (elimination)
5. **Expected Output Generation** — Generate ground-truth answers for training and evaluation

A critical design principle: synthetic data must mirror your production retrieval logic (chunk size, overlap, tokenization) to be useful.

## Quality Filtering: The Decisive Factor

Quality filtering separates successful synthetic data efforts from model-degrading ones. The [[concepts/data-quality-bottleneck]] principle applies with special force: low-quality synthetic data actively harms model performance rather than merely failing to help.

**Multi-dimensional filtering rubrics** evaluate:
- Clarity and comprehensibility
- Depth and original insights
- Structural organization
- Topic relevance and accuracy
- Novelty and diversity

**Automated quality tools** include:
- LLM-as-judge evaluation
- Reward model scoring (e.g., Nemotron-4-340B assesses helpfulness, correctness, coherence, complexity, verbosity)
- Decontamination checks to prevent benchmark leakage

## The "Textbook Quality" Breakthrough

Microsoft's Phi model series (see [[sources/textbooks-are-all-you-need-phi]]) demonstrated that data quality decisively outperforms data quantity:
- Phi-1 (1.3B params, 7B tokens) rivals models trained on 100x more data
- GPT-3.5-generated synthetic "textbooks" and exercises form 1/7 of training but disproportionately drive quality
- Iterative generate-filter-regenerate loops build progressively better corpora

## Risks and Mitigations

### Model Collapse
Training on synthetic data from models trained on synthetic data creates a degenerative feedback loop. Each generation amplifies errors and narrows the distribution. Mitigation: always maintain a human-verified "Golden Corpus" as anchor (see [[concepts/model-collapse]]).

### Benchmark Contamination
Synthetic data generated by powerful models may unintentionally contain rephrased benchmark examples. Decontamination via embedding similarity search is essential.

### Homogenization
LLM-generated data tends toward similar styles and patterns. Query evolution and explicit diversity requirements help but don't eliminate this risk.

## Connection to LLM Knowledge Bases

Synthetic data generation is directly relevant to [[concepts/llm-knowledge-base]] in two ways:

1. **Knowledge base → training data**: A well-maintained wiki can serve as high-quality source material for generating synthetic fine-tuning data, as suggested by [[entities/andrej-karpathy]]
2. **Training data → better models**: Domain-specific synthetic data enables fine-tuning models that are better at maintaining and querying the knowledge base itself

This creates a virtuous cycle: better KB → better synthetic data → better models → better KB maintenance.
