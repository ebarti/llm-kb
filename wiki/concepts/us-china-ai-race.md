## Overview

The US-China AI race is the central axis of [[concepts/ai-geopolitics]], encompassing competition across model capability, hardware, capital, talent, military applications, regulatory frameworks, and global influence. By 2026, this competition has moved from abstract strategic concern to concrete policy confrontation with measurable dimensions.

## Key Dynamics

### The DeepSeek Shock

[[entities/deepseek]]'s R1 model was the defining event of 2025 in this race. By matching OpenAI's o1 performance at a fraction of the compute cost, DeepSeek demonstrated that algorithmic efficiency can partially offset hardware disadvantage. NVIDIA lost $600 billion in market value in a single day. CSIS assessed it as "not a Sputnik moment, yet" but acknowledged the gap has meaningfully narrowed ([[sources/csis-deepseek-breakthrough-redefining-ai-race]]).

### Hardware Asymmetry

The [[concepts/ai-chip-export-controls]] dimension remains the US's strongest lever. [[entities/nvidia]]'s chips are 5x more powerful than [[entities/huawei]]'s best (widening to 17x by 2027). Huawei produces only 4-5% of Nvidia's output. However, the Trump administration's decision to allow H200 chip exports could provide China with computing power it couldn't produce domestically until 2028-2029 ([[sources/cfr-china-ai-chip-deficit-huawei-nvidia]]).

### China's Ecosystem Advantage

China's AI success is not just about individual companies but about a comprehensive [[concepts/ai-industrial-policy]] ecosystem: 2,100+ government guidance funds ($1.86T target size), provincial competition driving decentralized innovation, computing vouchers across 17 provinces, and B2G partnerships creating funding-data feedback loops ([[sources/lawfare-china-ai-ecosystem-beyond-deepseek]]).

### Military Dimension

The [[concepts/ai-arms-race]] has produced operational results. The US demonstrated 900 AI-enabled strikes in 12 hours during Operation Epic Fury, while China's PLA pursues "fully autonomous execution of combat decisions" but has zero combat testing. The US advantage lies in integrated systems and operational experience rather than any single technology ([[sources/nanonets-ai-warfare-pentagon-china-2026]]).

### Open Source as Strategy

China's embrace of [[concepts/open-source-vs-closed-ai]] (DeepSeek R1 under MIT license, Qwen, others) serves multiple strategic purposes: building global developer trust, democratizing access to reduce US moat, and accelerating the broader AI ecosystem. Chinese open-source AI models jumped from 1.2% of global usage in late 2024 to nearly 30% by end of 2025.

## The Specialization Thesis

Rather than one side achieving total victory, the race is trending toward specialization ([[sources/time-us-china-ai-race-graphs]]). The US excels in frontier model research, compute infrastructure, and capital concentration. China excels in algorithmic efficiency, state coordination, open-source deployment, and application-level innovation within its domestic market.

## Open Questions

- Can the seven-month quality gap be sustained as efficiency improvements reduce compute requirements?
- Will relaxed export controls prove to be a strategic error providing China a "2-3 year boost"?
- Does China's open-source strategy create a more durable competitive advantage than US closed-source moats?
- How does the military dimension change if AI systems achieve full autonomy?
