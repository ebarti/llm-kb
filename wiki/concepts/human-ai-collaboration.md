## Overview

Human-AI collaboration in knowledge creation spans a spectrum from AI as passive tool (spell-checking, search) through AI as research assistant (finding sources, identifying gaps) to AI as active participant (generating content, synthesizing across sources) to AI as autonomous agent (writing articles independently). The COHUMAIN framework ([[sources/cohumain-collective-intelligence-human-ai]]) studies how these different levels of AI involvement affect [[concepts/collective-intelligence]].

## Key Ideas

### The COHUMAIN Framework

Gupta, Nguyen, Gonzalez, and Woolley propose **Collective Human-Machine Intelligence** as an interdisciplinary research domain. Their central question: "How do we know that a sociotechnical system as a whole is exhibiting collective intelligence?" Their answer involves four core problems spanning metacognition (understanding each other's mental states and knowledge) and interaction (aligning mental states and coordinating actions).

### A Sobering Baseline

A key empirical finding: **human-only teams consistently outperform human-AI teams** in information sharing efficiency. Current AI design fails to integrate with collective cognitive processes — AI agents lack the social intelligence and shared context that makes human teamwork effective.

This suggests that simply inserting AI into collaborative processes is insufficient — or counterproductive. The design challenge is creating AI that interfaces smoothly with human [[concepts/transactive-memory-systems]] rather than disrupting them.

### Three Ways AI Can Harm Collective Intelligence

1. **Memory displacement**: When AI handles information retrieval, humans stop forming their own memories — undermining the distributed knowledge that makes groups intelligent
2. **Autonomy removal**: AI micromanagement reduces human agency and intrinsic motivation
3. **Over-reliance**: Groups that depend on AI facilitation lose the capacity for independent collective reasoning

### The Vibe Teaming Model

Taylor and Page ([[sources/brookings-ai-collective-intelligence]]) propose a more promising approach: AI as **translation engine** rather than participant. AI scaffolds human performance — capturing deliberation, connecting to models, creating feedback loops — without replacing human judgment. This "vibe teaming" approach treats AI as infrastructure, not colleague.

### Wikipedia's Empirical Answer

Wikipedia's experience provides the largest natural experiment in human-AI knowledge collaboration:
- **AI as research assistant**: 87% of editors found AI helpful for identifying gaps and finding sources ([[sources/wikiedu-ai-wikipedia-editing-2025]])
- **AI as content generator**: Two-thirds of AI-generated articles failed verification
- **AI as autonomous agent**: Tom-Assistant incident provoked community backlash

The lesson: AI works best in collaborative knowledge creation when it augments human editorial judgment (research, detection, quality assessment) rather than replacing it (content generation, article creation).

### The Goal Autonomy Problem

Unlike human collaborators, AI agents lack higher-order goal autonomy — their objectives are set by designers, not chosen by the agent. This creates a fundamental misalignment risk in collaborative settings: the AI optimizes for its designer's objectives (which may be engagement, revenue, or training data extraction) rather than the group's knowledge-building goals.

## Design Principles for Effective Human-AI Knowledge Collaboration

Based on the research:

1. **Augment, don't replace**: AI for research assistance, quality checking, and translation — not content generation
2. **Preserve human agency**: Ensure humans maintain decision-making authority over knowledge artifacts
3. **Support transactive memory**: Help people know who knows what, without displacing their own learning
4. **Maintain moderate autonomy**: Neither fully autonomous AI nor mere tool — moderate AI autonomy proves most effective
5. **Ensure transparency**: Make AI contributions visible and distinguishable from human contributions
