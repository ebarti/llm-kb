## Overview

Progressive Summarization is [[entities/tiago-forte]]'s technique for distilling notes into their essence through multiple passes over time. Rather than summarizing once (which requires you to know what's important before you've used the note), Progressive Summarization layers highlighting across multiple revisits, each time surfacing what matters most for your current context.

## The Four Layers

- **Layer 0**: The original, full-length source text
- **Layer 1**: Content initially captured into your note-taking system (already somewhat curated by the act of saving)
- **Layer 2 — Bolding**: The first real summarization. Bold keywords, key phrases, and key sentences that represent the core of the idea. This should be doable in a quick skim.
- **Layer 3 — Highlighting**: The "best of the best." Only highlight passages among the bolded text that are truly unique, surprising, or valuable. This creates a scannable executive layer.
- **Layer 4 — Executive Summary**: Going beyond highlighting others' words to recording your own. For the most insightful notes, write a brief informal summary at the top restating the key points in your own words.

## Design Philosophy

"What you are doing as you add layers is structuring your attentional environment, creating affordances and signposts that signal to your Future Self where to concentrate their attention for maximum return."

The technique is designed for the reality of knowledge work: you rarely know at capture time what will be important later. Progressive Summarization defers that judgment, adding value incrementally with each encounter rather than demanding perfection upfront. This is what Forte calls **Opportunistic Distillation** — add a layer whenever you happen to revisit a note, not in a dedicated processing session.

## Relationship to Other Techniques

Progressive Summarization occupies a different niche than [[concepts/zettelkasten]] or [[concepts/evergreen-notes]]. Those systems emphasize *creating new atomic notes in your own words*. Progressive Summarization works *within* captured source material, creating layers of attention that make future retrieval faster. The approaches are complementary: you might use Progressive Summarization on a captured article, then extract the key insights into evergreen notes or Zettel.
