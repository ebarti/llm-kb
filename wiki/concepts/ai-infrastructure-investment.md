## Overview

The scale of investment in AI physical infrastructure in 2025-2026 is historically unprecedented. Gartner projects worldwide AI spending at $2.5 trillion for 2026 — a 44% increase over 2025. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang estimates $3-4 trillion will be spent on AI infrastructure by the end of the decade. The investment has become large enough to reshape macroeconomics: the Federal Reserve published a formal analysis of AI's impact on global trade flows in February 2026.

This is not merely a technology investment story. It is an infrastructure buildout comparable to railroads, electrification, or telecommunications — a physical transformation of the world to support a new computational paradigm.

### What the Money Buys

1. **Data center construction**: Land, buildings, power delivery, cooling systems. New campuses are measured in gigawatts — the Stargate Initiative at $500 billion is the single largest project.

2. **Chip procurement**: [[entities/nvidia]] Blackwell GPUs are the primary purchase, along with [[entities/google-tpu]]s (internally), Amazon Trainium (internally), and [[entities/amd]] MI300 series.

3. **Power infrastructure**: Nuclear plants (Microsoft-Constellation 2 GW), solar farms (Amazon 1.5 GW Texas), grid upgrades. Energy is becoming the binding constraint. See [[concepts/ai-data-center-energy]].

4. **Networking**: InfiniBand, optical interconnects, co-packaged optics for connecting GPU clusters at scale.

5. **Cooling**: Liquid cooling systems reaching 47% adoption as chip TDP exceeds 1,000W.

### Key Deals

### The Startup Layer

Foundational AI startups raised $178 billion in Q1 2026 alone — double the $88.9 billion raised in all of 2025. OpenAI's round reached $122 billion. [[entities/anthropic]] raised $30 billion at a $380 billion valuation. [[entities/cerebras]] targets a $22B IPO in Q2 2026. The capital concentration is extreme: a handful of companies absorb the majority of investment.

### The Bubble-or-Foundation Debate

The central question is whether AI revenue growth will justify the investment. The comparison cuts both ways:
- **Bubble case**: Historical precedent (dot-com, fiber optic) shows technology infrastructure can be massively overbuilt relative to near-term demand
- **Foundation case**: AI is already generating revenue at scale, and compute demand consistently exceeds supply. The IEA's upward revision of energy projections suggests demand is accelerating, not decelerating

### Geopolitical Dimensions

US export controls on advanced chips create geographic imbalances in AI infrastructure. China is investing in domestic chip development (CXMT HBM, Huawei Ascend). The Middle East is emerging as a new AI infrastructure hub (Cerebras's Stargate UAE project in Abu Dhabi). The Federal Reserve's analysis of AI's global trade effects reflects the macroeconomic significance of these flows.
