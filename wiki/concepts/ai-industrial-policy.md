## Overview

AI industrial policy encompasses the range of government strategies, investments, and regulatory frameworks nations use to build and maintain AI capabilities. In the context of [[concepts/ai-geopolitics]], industrial policy has become the primary mechanism through which states compete for AI leadership.

## Contrasting Approaches

### China: State-Coordinated Ecosystem

China operates the most comprehensive AI industrial policy globally ([[sources/lawfare-china-ai-ecosystem-beyond-deepseek]]):

- **Strategic plan**: State Council AI Development Plan (2017) targeting 1 trillion yuan AI economy by 2030
- **Capital**: Over 2,100 government guidance funds with $1.86 trillion in target size
- **Direct investment**: 1 trillion yuan ($137 billion) over five years for AI supply chain
- **Provincial competition**: Local governments compete for AI prestige, driving decentralized innovation
- **Subsidies**: Computing vouchers (~$300K/company) across 17 provinces
- **B2G partnerships**: Hangzhou's City Brain creates funding-data feedback loops
- **Tax incentives**: "National high-tech enterprise" (HTNE) designations for preferential treatment

**Outcome**: DeepSeek emerged from this ecosystem — transitioning from hedge fund to AI lab after government crackdowns redirected capital toward AI.

### United States: Private-Sector Led with Government Support

- **Stargate Project**: $500 billion private AI infrastructure investment (announced 2025)
- **National AI Research Institutes**: $2.8 billion federal investment
- **Export controls**: [[concepts/ai-chip-export-controls]] as primary competitive tool
- **DARPA and military procurement**: Project Maven ($480M), GenAI.mil
- **Tax policy**: CSIS advocates tax incentives for private open-source AI development

**Outcome**: US advantage comes primarily from private capital concentration ($250B Q1 2026) and existing tech infrastructure rather than state direction.

### European Union: Regulatory Industrial Policy

- **Digital Europe Programme**: EUR 1.6 billion
- **EU AI Act**: Uses regulation as a competitive tool via the "Brussels Effect"
- **Talent retention**: Struggling — net tech talent inflows halved
- **Strategic weakness**: Neither sufficient public investment nor competitive private capital markets

## How It Connects

- **[[concepts/ai-sovereignty]]**: Industrial policy is the implementation mechanism for sovereignty ambitions

## Open Questions

- Does China's state-directed approach produce more efficient outcomes or more misallocated capital?
- Can the US maintain AI leadership through private capital alone?
- Is the EU's regulatory approach a substitute for or complement to direct investment?
