## Overview

AI drug discovery uses machine learning, generative chemistry, and computational structural biology to accelerate the identification of drug targets, design of molecular candidates, and optimization of clinical development. As of early 2026, the field is transitioning from proof-of-concept to regulatory validation, with 173+ AI-discovered programs in clinical development and 15-20 entering Phase III trials.

## Key Methods

### Target Identification
- AI analyzes biological data to identify disease-relevant protein targets.
- [[entities/alphafold]] 3 enables understanding of target-drug interactions at the molecular level.
- [[entities/alphagenome]] identifies disease-causing genetic variants in non-coding regions.

### Molecular Design
- **Generative chemistry** ([[entities/insilico-medicine]]): AI generates novel molecular structures from scratch.
- **Physics-based AI** (Schrodinger): Combines quantum mechanics with ML for precise binding prediction.
- **Phenomics** ([[entities/recursion-pharmaceuticals]]): High-throughput biological imaging (2.2M experiments/week) to identify molecular effects.
- **De novo protein design** ([[entities/rfdiffusion]]): Atomically accurate antibody and protein therapeutic design.

### Clinical Optimization
- AI-powered patient stratification for clinical trials.
- Predictive modeling for dosing and adverse effects.
- AI-assisted regulatory submission preparation.

## Evidence and Metrics

- **Phase I success rate**: 80-90% for AI-discovered compounds (vs 52% historical average).
- **Timeline compression**: Insilico achieved 30-month target-to-Phase-I (vs traditional 6-8 years).
- **Pipeline scale**: 173+ programs in clinical development; $5-7B market (2025).
- **Failures**: Compounds failed in 2023 trials (atopic dermatitis, schizophrenia, cancer); REC-994 discontinued May 2025.
- **No FDA approval yet** as of December 2025. First approval projected 2026-2027 (~60% probability).

## Regulatory Landscape

- FDA draft guidance (January 2025): risk-based credibility assessment for AI models in drug development.
- Joint FDA-EMA guiding principles (January 2026): regulatory coordination for AI drug submissions.
- Final guidance expected 2026.

## Why 2026 Matters

Phase III is where most drug candidates historically fail. The 15-20 AI programs entering Phase III in 2026 will provide the first serious evidence base for whether AI's early advantages (higher Phase I success, faster timelines) translate into actual approved drugs. This will determine whether AI drug discovery is a genuine paradigm shift or an overhyped acceleration of the same failure-prone process.

## Open Questions

- Do higher Phase I success rates translate to higher Phase III success rates?
- Can AI address the "biology risk" that causes most late-stage failures?
- Will regulators require new frameworks for AI-designed molecules?
- How will IP law handle AI-generated molecular designs?
