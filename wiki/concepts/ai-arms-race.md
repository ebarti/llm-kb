## Overview

The AI arms race refers to the accelerating competition between nations — primarily the US and China — to develop, deploy, and integrate artificial intelligence into military systems. Unlike previous technology races, the AI arms race operates across a uniquely broad front: targeting, intelligence analysis, logistics, cyber operations, autonomous systems, and decision support.

By 2026, AI has moved from military research concept to operational deployment in active combat, fundamentally changing the speed and scale of military operations.

## Current State of Military AI

### United States

The Pentagon has built an integrated AI stack ([[sources/nanonets-ai-warfare-pentagon-china-2026]]):

- **Project Maven**: $480 million Army contract, deployed across five combatant commands. Maven Smart System fuses satellite imagery, geolocation, and communications intercepts
- **GenAI.mil**: Pentagon-wide platform with xAI's Grok models at classified levels (December 2025)
- **Targeting systems**: Gospel (target buildings), Lavender (individual combatants), "Where's Daddy" (phone tracking)
- **Operational partners**: Palantir, OpenAI, xAI

**Key operational demonstration**: Operation Epic Fury (February 28 - March 5, 2026) — 900 AI-enabled strikes in 12 hours across 24 of Iran's 31 provinces. This compressed weeks of planning into hours.

### China (PLA)

- Doctrinal framework: "intelligentized warfare" — AI as the organizing principle for the entire future military
- Building AI decision-support systems to compensate for perceived command weaknesses (Georgetown CSET review)
- March 2025: PLA-linked researchers described "fully autonomous execution of combat decisions" as development goal
- Algorithmic sovereignty via [[entities/huawei]] chip integration and DeepSeek efficiency
- **Critical gap**: Zero combat testing since the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War

### Other Nations

- **India**: AI-enabled Meteorological Reporting System (Project Anumaan) used during 2025 India-Pakistan conflict for artillery targeting up to 200km
- **Israel**: Expanded AI military use with "Operational Data and AI Factory" for drone strikes and targeting in conflicts with Iran and Lebanon
- **Humanoid robots**: Foundation's Phantom robot under development for defense; $24M in contracts with US Army, Navy, and Air Force

## The Speed Asymmetry

The most significant finding from Operation Epic Fury is the compression of the military decision cycle. AI-enabled targeting reduced planning timelines from days/weeks to hours. This creates a strategic asymmetry: the side with better AI integration can operate at a tempo its adversary cannot match — analogous to Britain's Chain Home radar advantage in 1940.

## Ethical and Strategic Concerns

- **Anthropic's removal from Pentagon contracts during active strikes** exposed the fragility of relying on private AI companies with ethical constraints
- **Autonomous targeting**: Systems like Lavender identify individuals for strikes with limited human oversight
- **No international treaty**: Unlike nuclear weapons, there is no arms control framework for AI weapons
- **Pressure to bypass safety**: The [[concepts/ai-arms-race]] creates incentives to deploy AI systems faster than safety research can validate them
