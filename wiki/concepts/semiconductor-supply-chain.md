## Overview

The semiconductor supply chain is a critical bottleneck in [[concepts/ai-geopolitics]]. AI chip design (led by [[entities/nvidia]]) and manufacturing (dominated by TSMC in Taiwan) are geographically separated, creating complex dependencies that shape the [[concepts/us-china-ai-race]].

## Key Actors

| Company | Role | Process Node | Location |
|---------|------|-------------|----------|
| TSMC | Manufacturing (foundry) | 3nm and below | Taiwan |
| SMIC | Manufacturing (foundry) | 7nm ceiling | China |
| Nvidia | Design (fabless) | N/A | US (manufactured by TSMC) |
| Huawei (HiSilicon) | Design | N/A | China (manufactured by SMIC) |
| ASML | Lithography equipment | EUV | Netherlands |

## The Manufacturing Gap

SMIC is constrained to 7nm process technology, while TSMC operates at 3nm and is advancing to 2nm. This 2+ generation gap means:
- [[entities/huawei]]'s chips are fundamentally less powerful per transistor
- The gap is widening, not closing (Huawei's 2026 next-gen chip regresses in performance)
- No amount of design innovation can fully compensate for manufacturing limitations
- China's linear production growth cannot match exponential AI demand

## Taiwan's Geopolitical Centrality

TSMC's dominance in advanced semiconductor manufacturing makes Taiwan perhaps the most geopolitically significant piece of real estate in the AI era. A disruption to TSMC's operations — through conflict, natural disaster, or political crisis — would halt the global AI infrastructure buildout. This creates a strategic calculus where all major AI powers have a vested interest in Taiwan's stability.
