## Overview

NeuroAI is the interdisciplinary research program arguing that neuroscience and AI should deeply inform each other. The field is built on a historical observation: many of AI's greatest successes were inspired by the brain — convolutional neural networks from the visual cortex (Hubel & Wiesel), reinforcement learning from dopamine reward signals, and attention mechanisms from cognitive science. NeuroAI proposes that the next breakthroughs will similarly come from engaging with biological intelligence.

## Key Ideas

### The Bidirectional Research Exchange

NeuroAI operates in two directions:
1. **Neuroscience → AI**: Biological principles (sparse coding, predictive processing, complementary learning systems, neuromodulation) inspire new AI architectures and algorithms
2. **AI → Neuroscience**: Deep learning provides tools for analyzing neural data, computational models of brain circuits, and testable theories of brain function

### Core Research Agenda

The NeuroAI consortium (Zador, Escola, Richards, et al., 2023) identifies six priority areas:

1. **Sample-efficient learning**: How does the brain learn from a handful of examples when AI needs millions? The answer involves innate structure, [[concepts/complementary-learning-systems]], and rich internal models.

2. **Embodiment and sensorimotor interaction**: Intelligence may require a body and environment — perception and action are inseparable in biology, unlike most AI architectures.

3. **Biological attention and memory**: The brain's attention mechanisms and multi-system memory architecture ([[concepts/complementary-learning-systems]], episodic/semantic/procedural memory) offer design principles for AI agents.

4. **Neural efficiency**: [[concepts/sparse-coding]] and the [[concepts/efficient-coding-hypothesis]] explain how the brain achieves extraordinary performance on 20 watts.

5. **Continual learning**: How brains solve [[concepts/catastrophic-forgetting]] through synaptic consolidation, sleep replay, and complementary learning systems.

6. **Reward and motivation**: Biological neuromodulation (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine) provides richer learning signals than scalar reward in RL.

### Historical Debt

| AI Innovation | Neuroscience Inspiration |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| CNNs | Hubel & Wiesel's visual cortex receptive fields |
| Reinforcement learning | Dopamine reward prediction error (Schultz) |
| Attention mechanisms | Cognitive attention and selective processing |
| Dropout regularization | Neuronal noise and stochastic firing |
| Memory-augmented networks | Hippocampal memory and retrieval |
| Generative models (VAEs) | [[concepts/free-energy-principle]] / predictive coding |

## Current State (2025-2026)

NeuroAI is gaining institutional momentum. The field has moved from scattered individual efforts to organized consortia, dedicated funding (NSF NeuroAI program), and industry labs (DeepMind, Anthropic, Meta FAIR) explicitly studying brain-inspired approaches. The commercialization of [[concepts/neuromorphic-computing]] (Intel Loihi 3, IBM NorthPole) represents the hardware arm of NeuroAI becoming market-ready.

## Open Questions

- What is the minimal set of neuroscience principles needed for "general" intelligence?
- Will NeuroAI converge with or diverge from the scaling paradigm?
