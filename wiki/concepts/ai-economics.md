## Overview

AI economics in 2026 is defined by a staggering asymmetry: investment at a scale unprecedented in technology history, with revenue that — while growing rapidly — has yet to justify the capital deployed. Global AI spending reached $2.52 trillion in 2026 (Gartner), with the four largest tech companies alone investing ~$700 billion in infrastructure. Whether this represents the early stages of the most transformative technology in human history or the largest speculative bubble ever created is the "trillion-dollar question."

## Key Ideas

### The Scale of Investment

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Global AI spending (2026) | $2.52 trillion | Gartner |
| Big Tech CapEx (2026) | ~$700 billion | Goldman Sachs |
| AI infrastructure by 2028 | ~$3 trillion cumulative | Morgan Stanley |
| Global AI market (2033) | $4.8 trillion | UN UNCTAD |
| Hardware share of spending | ~59% | Gartner |

The composition matters: hardware and infrastructure dominate at 59%, meaning the bulk of spending flows into data centers, GPUs, power infrastructure, and cooling systems — not software or services.

### GDP and Productivity Impact

The St. Louis Fed finds AI has surpassed IT's contribution to real GDP growth during the dot-com boom. Net AI-related investment accounts for 40-50 basis points (20-25%) of US real GDP growth in 2025. 80% of the increase in private domestic demand in H1 2025 is attributable to data centers and high-tech spending.

Long-term projections (Dallas Fed / Penn Wharton):
- +1.5% GDP by 2035
- +3% by 2055
- +3.7% by 2075
- Measurable GDP impact starting 2027

### The Revenue Gap

Despite massive investment, consumption (not AI investment) was the biggest driver of US GDP in 2025. The ratio of AI CapEx to AI revenue generated is a persistent concern. This echoes previous technology cycles — the dot-com era saw similar investment-to-revenue gaps before eventual correction and recovery.

[[entities/leopold-aschenbrenner]] frames this differently: the investment is justified because AGI (arriving ~2027 in his thesis) would render all prior investments trivially profitable. The infrastructure being built is not for current AI but for the intelligence explosion that follows.

### The Infrastructure Buildout

The physical scale is remarkable:
- Data centers consumed ~415 TWh in 2024 (1.5% global electricity)
- Projected to double to ~945 TWh by 2030 (3% of global electricity)
- AI data centers could need 68 GW by 2027 — near California's entire capacity
- GPU lead times: 36-52 weeks; NVIDIA Rubin capped at 200-300K units for 2026
- Memory chip shortage: AI consumes 70% of DRAM

See [[concepts/ai-energy-and-infrastructure]] for the full energy picture.

### Aschenbrenner's Trillion-Dollar Framing

[[sources/aschenbrenner-situational-awareness]] predicts "trillions of dollars will go into GPU, datacenter, and power buildout before the end of the decade." He frames this not as speculation but as inevitable national security investment — comparable to Cold War defense spending. American electricity production must expand "tens of percent."

### Amodei's Cautious Economics

[[sources/amodei-machines-of-loving-grace]] is more cautious on economics than on biology or health. His dream scenario of 20% annual growth in developing nations acknowledges that economic systems involve human constraints (corruption, institutions, politics) that resist optimization even with superhuman intelligence.
