## Overview

The "path to AGI" is the central question in AI as of 2026: when will AI systems match or exceed human-level general intelligence, and through what mechanisms? The answer depends critically on what AGI means — a question on which there is no consensus — and on which of several scaling paradigms proves most productive. Three influential thinkers have offered strikingly different framings: [[entities/leopold-aschenbrenner]] argues for aggressive timelines driven by compute extrapolation, [[entities/dario-amodei]] focuses on what powerful AI could achieve, and [[entities/ilya-sutskever]] contends the scaling era is ending and novel research is required.

## Key Ideas

### The Timeline Landscape

Expert predictions have compressed dramatically. In 2020, the median estimate was 2060-2070. By 2026, Metaculus forecasters put the median at ~2033 (50% probability), with AI company leaders clustering around 2026-2028.

### Three Competing Theories of Progress

**1. Compute Extrapolation (Aschenbrenner)**
GPT-2 to GPT-4: preschooler to smart high-schooler in 4 years, driven by ~3-4 OOMs more compute + ~1-2 OOMs algorithmic efficiency. Another equivalent jump by 2027 reaches AGI. This view emphasizes the reliable predictive power of [[concepts/compute-scaling]] trends.

**2. Marginal Returns to Intelligence (Amodei)**
Rather than predicting when, Amodei asks what "powerful AI" could achieve across domains. His framework analyzes which constraints (physical speed, data quality, institutional friction, physical laws) limit progress when intelligence becomes superhuman. This side-steps the definitional debate.

**3. Research-Driven Breakthroughs (Sutskever)**
The pre-training scaling paradigm is exhausted. Breakthroughs now require novel learning methods — not more GPUs. Current systems fail at basic generalization because they lack evolutionary priors and internal value systems. This view implies longer timelines but potentially more transformative outcomes.

### The Definitional Problem

Perhaps the most important meta-finding: "AGI" means radically different things to different experts, making predictions nearly non-comparable. DeepMind uses a five-tier capability framework. OpenAI has a five-level internal model. Anthropic avoids the term entirely. LeCun rejects the concept as applied to language models.

This suggests the question "when will we get AGI?" may be less useful than "when will AI systems be able to do X?" — where X is a specific economically or scientifically meaningful task.

## Open Questions

- Is the pre-training scaling paradigm truly exhausted, or will new data sources (synthetic, multimodal) reignite it?
- Will test-time compute (o1/R1 paradigm) prove a true new scaling dimension or a temporary workaround?
- Does Sutskever's "age of research" imply longer timelines, or could a breakthrough produce a sudden jump?
- Is the concept of "AGI" useful at all, or should we focus on specific capability thresholds?
- Can the massive infrastructure buildout ($700B+ Big Tech CapEx in 2026) actually accelerate timelines?
