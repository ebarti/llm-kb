## Overview

Generative chemistry uses AI to design novel molecular structures with desired pharmaceutical properties, rather than screening existing chemical libraries. This represents a shift from "finding" molecules to "creating" them, dramatically expanding the chemical space explored during drug development.

## Methods

### Generative Models (Insilico Medicine)
- AI generates entirely new molecular structures optimized for target binding, selectivity, and drug-like properties.
- Identifies both disease targets and therapeutic molecules.
- Achieved 30-month target-to-Phase-I timeline for rentosertib.

### Physics-Based AI (Schrodinger)
- Combines quantum mechanics simulations with machine learning.
- Predicts precise molecular binding interactions.
- Zasocitinib (TAK-279) in Phase III for psoriasis.

### AI Retrosynthesis
- Predicts the synthetic route to manufacture a designed molecule.
- Three model types: template-based, semi-template, and template-free.
- Yale's AI model enables direct multi-step retrosynthesis planning.
- ChemEnzyRetroPlanner combines organic and enzymatic strategies.

### Phenomics (Recursion)
- High-throughput biological imaging (2.2M experiments/week).
- AI analyzes cellular phenotype changes to identify molecular effects.
- Screens for unexpected biological activity at massive scale.

## Relationship to Protein Design

Generative chemistry (small molecules) and [[concepts/ai-protein-design]] (proteins) are complementary approaches to drug design:
- Small molecules: oral bioavailability, well-understood pharmacology, but limited to protein surfaces.
- Designed proteins (antibodies, etc.): access to broader target space, higher specificity, but require injection and are expensive to manufacture.
